# Spec: Food Truck (needs and wants, goods and services, jobs, spending; build a food truck and serve)

Status: built on 2026-11-18 from `brief.md` (pass 1 of 3: build), playable at `/food-truck/` with a tile in **Learning Games** on the
landing page, after Dino Dig. Every decision below is a **default for the parent to confirm**. Not yet run on a real iPad or in Safari.
The name is still the brief's working name. The bug pass and the visual design pass follow; the look is the family look in street-fair
colours, not yet designed.

A new learning game on the family site, built the same way as Turbo Tally and Rover Code (`src/turbo-tally/` was the closest model: a
non-reading game of taps, with a Tune-Up and Meetings), for the same player (6 to 7, reads numbers and short words, leans on the recorded
voice, alone on an iPad 9). Short mixed Rounds earn Bolts, spent on trucks and parts. Each Round also earns a **Service**: customers walk
up to his truck and he makes their food with his hands.

**The theme (open question 1).** The brief asks the parent whether a food truck appeals to him at all (he has asked for robots, rovers and
race cars, never food). It was built as briefed; the six kinds don't depend on it, and the same questions would sit under a bike shop,
a pit-lane parts shop or a space trading post. To make it his kind of thing anyway, the Regulars include a robot (Robo), an alien, a T.
rex, a shark and an astronaut, and the mascots include a robot, a rocket, a dinosaur and a dragon.

## Research: what first graders are taught about economics

| Virginia SOL 1.7, 1.8, 1.9 (2015) | goods and services, people as consumers and producers; people make choices because they cannot have everything they want; saving means giving up spending now to buy later | Goods and Services, Jobs Level 3 (maker and shopper), Need or Want Level 3, Save Up |

| California HSS 1.6.1, 1.6.2 | exchange and the use of money to buy goods and services; the specialised work people do to make goods and services | Add It Up, Enough?, Jobs |

Sources: 19 TAC §113.12 (Social Studies, Grade 1, adopted 2022) and §111.3 (Mathematics, Grade 1), read on law.cornell.edu; Virginia's
2015 History and Social Science Standards of Learning and curriculum framework (doe.virginia.gov); Georgia's Social Studies GSE (as
listed by Cobb, Muscogee and Marietta schools; the georgiastandards.org PDF itself was not opened); California's HSS-1.6.1 on the CDE
standards database. The Georgia and Virginia wording is paraphrased from those listings, not quoted.

**What the research changed:** nothing in the kinds; the brief's six cover the strand. Two things it confirmed: Jobs Level 3 (who made it,
who bought it) is the producer/consumer idea Georgia and Virginia name, and Save Up Level 2 ("You earn $2 a day") is the TEKS idea that
money earned is income. One thing it found and left out: TEKS §111.3(b)(9)(D), "consider charitable giving", is a matter of opinion, which
Save Up must never ask (open question 8). Monster Truck Money owns coins and cents (TEKS §111.3(b)(4)); this game is whole dollars only.

## Kinds

Six kinds, three Levels each, with Stretch inside a Level (more choices of the other sort, closer prices, bigger amounts). Every answer
is a tap; a sort is a tap on a picture (the first one left is picked for him) and then a tap on its shelf.

| Kind | Opens | Level 1 | Level 2 | Level 3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Need or Want** `need` | start | two pictures, a need and a want: "Tap the one you need." (from Stretch 1, one time in three "Tap the one you want.") | four to sort onto the heart shelf and the star shelf (two and two; from Stretch 2, one to three needs) | "You have $7. You need lunch and you want a kite. Which do you buy first?" Each price is within his money, the two together are not |
| **Goods and Services** `goods` | start | "A good is a thing you can hold. Tap the good." (from Stretch 1, sometimes "Tap the service.") | four to sort into the basket and by the helping hands | "Who gives a service?" / "Who makes a good?" from three workers |
| **Jobs** `job` | Goods and Services L2 | "Who helps when you are sick?" from two workers | "What does the farmer use?" from three tools; from Stretch 1, also "Who uses a hammer?" from three workers | one picture of a market stall, a maker and a shopper: "Who baked the bread?" / "Who bought the bread?" |
| **Add It Up** `total` | start | the order circled on a menu board of four: two things, total to $10 (to $8 at Stretch 0) | two things, total $11 to $20 | three things, total to $20 |
| **Enough?** `enough` | Add It Up L2 | "You have $8. The taco costs $5. Do you have enough?" 👍 / 👎 (about one yes in four is exactly enough) | "You have $6. Which one can you buy?" three things, exactly one in reach (close prices from Stretch 1) | "You have $12. The pizza costs $7. How much is left?" |
| **Save Up** `save` | Enough? L2 | "The kite costs $10. You have $6. How many more do you need?" | "You earn $2 a day. How many days until you have $10?" (from Stretch 2, sometimes starting with some) | "You have $9. The ball costs $3. The kite costs $7. Can you buy both?" 👍 / 👎 (each alone within his money) |

The brief's Save Up Level 3 ("If you buy the gum, can you still buy the ball?") is asked as "Can you buy both?", which is the same sum
with fewer words to chain; there's no gum emoji, so the things are toys.

**The rules the tests enforce** (`econ/problems.test.ts`, 8,100 generated questions, every kind × Level × Stretch × 150 seeds):

- **Exactly one choice is right**, in every kind: every choice is tried; for a sort, every shelf for every thing.
- **Save Up never asks an opinion**: its only forms are how many more, how many days and can you buy both, and each answer is
  recomputed from the numbers (the yes and no of "both" come up about evenly).
- **Need or Want uses only the hand-checked table** below: needs are food, water, a home, clothes and medicine; wants are toys, games
  and sweets; no word on the arguable list (phone, car, bike, bicycle, pet, dog, cat, puppy, tablet, computer, TV, television, book,
  glasses, bed, soap, toothbrush, juice, internet, bus) in any name; no thing or picture on both lists.
- **Goods and Services uses only clear cases**: no word that makes a thing both (meal, dinner, lunch, breakfast, restaurant, pizza, taco,
  burger, house, delivery); no thing on both lists; no two services drawn by the same kind of worker in one sort.
- **Jobs shows no worker in a way that suggests who can do the job**: a worker's look (woman, man or person, and one of five skin tones)
  is picked at random and never from the job (`randomLook` takes no job), and across the generated questions every job comes up with all
  fifteen looks. Each job has its own tool, never the thing its emoji already holds.
- **"Which one can you buy?"** has exactly one thing costing his money or less.
- **Totals, what's left and every amount stay within 20**, in whole dollars; number choices are three different numbers from 1 to 20.
- **Number buttons are not read out**: the number choices draw without any voice at all (the test calls them with no voice to hand).
  Picture choices aren't read out either; the lessons name them.

**Unlock order (default).** Need or Want, Goods and Services and Add It Up are open from the start. Jobs opens at Goods and Services
Level 2, Enough? at Add It Up Level 2, Save Up at Enough? Level 2.

**Add It Up kept small (default).** It overlaps Math Bots' addition, so a Round asks it at most once (a missed one still comes back), and
it is the one kind with a **Tune-Up**: the first Rounds' one Add It Up is a probe at Level 2, three clean probes start it at Level 2
(which opens Enough?), two misses stop the row. It's never the first question of all.

**Meetings (default).** Before the first question that needs it, he meets the idea: "Needs are things you must have to live. Wants are
things that are nice to have." (with a heart shelf and a star shelf), "Goods are things you can hold. Services are jobs people do for
you." (a basket and helping hands), and the money ("This is a one-dollar coin." / "five-dollar bill" / "ten-dollar bill"). A Meeting
counts as one of the Round's five; at most two a Round. A new player's very first question is Need or Want, after its Meeting.

**Mastery and Rounds (as Rover Code and Turbo Tally).** 16 of the last 20 right on the first try, or 10 in a row; the quick rule (8 of
10, or 6 in a row) for a kind's first 30 questions at his Level. Never down. A star at Level 3; a starred kind comes back about once a
Round. Five answers, mixed across every open kind, never the same kind twice running while there's another. About one in three, and
always the one after a miss, is easier and doesn't count. A miss comes back later as a new question of the same kind and Level, no
Mastery credit. Bolts: one per first-try answer plus one for finishing (`boltsFor`). A Round resumes after a reload (`food-truck-place`).

### Money

Whole dollars only, drawn in code: gold $1 coins and green $5 and $10 bills, an amount shown with the fewest pieces and its number
(he reads numbers). Prices are tags ("$7"). The voice chains dollar amounts from the number clips ("You have", "8", "dollars."), as
Monster Truck Money chains prices. The voice says **bill**, not note (a US first grader's word, as Monster Truck Money says "$5 Bill").
None of Monster Truck Money's Game Modes (paying an exact price, counting coins, making change) is repeated: there are no cents, no
coin names beyond the $1 coin, and he never pays by tapping coins.

## Teaching a miss

Never a red X: a wrong choice greys and shakes. The first miss gets "Not quite!", one line pointing at what to look at, and "Try
again!". The second (or the first when he's been missing a lot, or 🔎) gets the whole lesson, ending with the right answer glowing:

- **Need or Want**: "You need clothes to stay warm. A balloon is fun, but you can live without it." A heart shelf and a star shelf appear
  under the pictures and the need slides onto the heart shelf, the want onto the star one. In a sort, the thing he misplaced bounces
  back to the counter, its line is said ("Needs go on the heart shelf.") and its shelf glows. Level 3 adds "Needs come first."
- **Goods and Services**: the good is picked up and carried (it floats, with 🤲 under it: "A ball is a good. You can hold it."); the
  service's worker works on what they serve ("A haircut is a service. Someone does it for you."). Level 3: each worker in turn gets their
  tool and "The farmer grows food. The farmer makes a good."
- **Jobs**: the worker is shown doing the job with the tool: the tool appears beside them and they work at it ("The doctor helps when you
  are sick. The doctor uses a stethoscope."). Level 3: "The baker baked the bread." then "The shopper bought the bread."
- **Add It Up**: the prices become rows of $1 coins ("Each coin is one dollar."), slide together into one row with the bigger price's
  coins already lit, and are counted on from it: "7. 8, 9, 10. That makes 10 dollars in all."
- **Enough?**: his coins line up over the price's coins, one to one ("Line up the coins."). Coins left over light up and are counted
  ("You have enough! ... That makes 5 dollars left."); a gap is drawn as empty circles, counted, and "Not enough. You need more."
  "Which one can you buy?" lines up his pick first, then the one he can afford.
- **Save Up**: the same two rows with the missing coins drawn as empty circles and counted ("You need 4 dollars."); days of earning
  stack up a row a day, counted by the running total ("2, 4, 6, 8, 10. That makes 5 days."); "Both together cost 10 dollars." then the
  rows.

## The reward: the Truck, the Service, the Spots and the Sticker Book

**The loop.** A finished Round earns Bolts and one **Service** (he starts with one free, so he sees it before his first purchase).
Bolts buy trucks and parts in the **Truck** shop. A Service pays **stars**, one for each customer he feeds, never Bolts; stars open
**Spots**. Each Service with a truck that still has a **Regular** he hasn't met brings that Regular, second in line; once fed, their
sticker goes in the **Sticker Book**. Bolts are not money: nothing in the reward is priced in dollars, and customers don't pay him.

**The Service (`service/service.ts` world, `service/render.ts` drawing, `service/ServiceScreen.tsx` screen; 2D canvas).** First a
card: "Where to?", the Spots (shut ones say how many stars they need, or that they need Lights), and Open!. Then, on a canvas the size of
the screen: the street on top (the Spot's sky, ground and things, customers walking up to the hatch, each with their order in a bubble:
the food and its toppings), the truck's awning and lights along the hatch, and the kitchen below: the grill spots (one with the Small
Grill, two with the Big Grill, three with the Mega Grill), each with the ticket of the customer it cooks for, and six trays (the base and
the truck's five toppings).
- **Drag the base** onto a grill spot (it snaps from 2.6 grill-widths away); **drag each topping** onto the food (it snaps from 1.7, or
  2.6 with the Magic Spatula); **hold** on the food to cook it (a ring fills in 1.2 seconds, half that with the Turbo Burner; lifting the
  finger keeps what it has); then **drag the food up to the customer** (anywhere in the street counts). The truck decides the order:
  a pizza or a smoothie gets its toppings first and then cooks; a burger, an ice cream, a hot dog, noodles, a donut, pancakes and popcorn
  cook first.
- A **tap** works too: a tray sends its thing to the food that needs it, ready food slides to its customer. (Default: drag is the way
  it's shown and said; tapping is there for a child whose drags keep slipping. Open question 5.)
- **A wrong topping bounces back to its tray** with a boing and "Look at the order!" ("Cook it first!" when it's right but early;
  "Add the toppings first!" when he holds to cook too soon). Nothing else can go wrong.
- **Help**: after 6 seconds without progress a 👇 points at the next thing and it's said ("Drag the tortilla over!", "Add the cheese",
  "Hold to bake!", "Give it to the customer!"); after 14 it happens by itself. After closing time those are 3 and 7.
- **Time**: customers come for 60 seconds (a ☀️ crosses the top); then "Closing time!", no one new comes, and everyone in line is still
  served. It ends when the last customer has walked off happy: "Great service! You fed 7 people!", a new Regular's sticker, his stars and
  any Spot that opened. Customers never get cross, never wait on a timer and never leave unfed.
- **Parts that change it**: the Grill (orders at once), the Awning (customers who can wait: 2, 3 or 4), the Speaker (a customer every 7
  seconds, down to every 4 with the Mega Speaker; each Speaker has its own jingle), the Lights (open the night Spots) and the Helpers:
  Bubble Machine (bubbles float up and waiting customers dance), Magic Spatula, Turbo Burner, Robot Helper (puts each base down for him)
  and Conveyor Belt (sends cooked food to the customer by itself). Each Helper can be switched off again in the shop.
- **It can't be failed** (`service/service.test.ts`): on every truck, with every Helper alone and all together, and with one, two and
  three grills, a child who plays properly feeds everyone who came (at least five in the minute, with nothing done for him), and a child
  who does nothing, or who taps trays and drops things anywhere, still finishes with everyone fed.
- **Sizes** (`service.test.ts`, at 810×1000, 1080×730, 390×760, 844×330, 1366×950 and 320×560): every tray and grill spot is at
  least 48px across, on screen, above the 56px floor that belongs to iPadOS, and never overlapping. The HUD (🏠, people fed, the sun) sits
  on the sky; 🏠 takes two taps during a Service.

## Save

`food-truck-save`, version 1 (an unknown version reads as a blank save), and `food-truck-place` for the screen and the Round in progress.
`readTruck` trusts nothing: only owned parts are fitted, only owned trucks driven, only open Spots parked at, unknown Regulars and
parts dropped, Helpers switched off only if owned. A Service isn't a place: a reload during one lands at the Truck with it still there.

## The voice

Recorded with `tools/make_voice.py` for `food-truck`, Aria (`en-US-AriaNeural`) at -8%: 735 clips, 9.3 MB (Dino Dig's are 11.1 MB).
Dollar amounts chain from the number clips ("You have", "8", "dollars."), as Monster Truck Money chains prices.

**The voice test.** Every clip was transcribed back with faster-whisper small.en, and the 112 doubtful or changed ones (every name,
the single words, the kind names in sentences, the tools and toppings) again with large-v3-turbo. What it changed:

- **Renamed** because a model heard them as another word: the mascots Cat ("Kate"), Duck ("deck"), Bear ("there") and Lion ("lyin'") are
  now Kitty Cat, Rubber Duck, Big Bear and Roaring Lion; the Tin Speaker ("10 speaker") is the Tiny Speaker; Bulb Lights ("bold lights")
  are Light Bulbs; Grape paint is Grape Purple; the Regulars Ollie ("Ali"), Ivy ("IV"), Nia ("near"), Gus ("gas") and Kai ("Thai") are
  Inky, Lucy, Nora, Grandpa Ben and Danny; kiwi ("Hiwi") is kiwi fruit.
- **Reworded**: "Who bakes bread?" ("Who baked spread") is "Who bakes the bread?"; "A need goes on the heart shelf." ("And need...") is
  "Needs go on the heart shelf."; "Services ready!" is "Services are ready!"; the star lines are "You are a star at Need or Want!" (with
  "a" or "an" before a kind, "an Add It Up" ran together as "an added-up").
- **Said with a breath**, in `COLLECTION_SPEECH['food-truck']` (this collection only): "Play more, Enough, to open this!", "You opened
  Enough!", "You are a star at Enough!" (the kind's question mark would be read as a question), "Play more Goods and Services, to open

  Sign" (heard "AeroSign") and "Grape, Purple" (heard "Great", as Turbo Tally found). Each fix was re-transcribed with large-v3-turbo

## Open questions for the parent

1. Does a food truck appeal to him at all? (The brief's own question; the kinds would fit a bike shop or a space trading post too.)
2. Read every row of the Need or Want and Goods and Services tables above. Especially: a bandage as medicine, a birthday cake as a want,
   rice and broccoli as food he'd recognise, and the service pictures (a car or a dog in a service picture).
3. Is "bill" right (the brief said notes)? Monster Truck Money says bill.
4. The baker is drawn as the cook emoji 🧑‍🍳 (there's no baker emoji). Clear enough?
5. Should tapping a tray (instead of dragging) stay? It's there for slipping fingers; it makes the Service quicker.
6. Is one Add It Up a Round right, and is a Tune-Up for it (and no other kind) right?
7. Is 2,491 Bolts of shop right? More can be added without raising payouts; the Taco Truck's four Regulars come quickly, then he needs
   a new truck for more.
8. TEKS §111.3(b)(9)(D), charitable giving, is left out because Save Up must never ask an opinion. Should there be anything about it?
9. Should the Service pay a Bolt or two (Dino Dig's Dig does)? It pays stars only, as briefed.
