## 1. Identity

- **Full name:** Lockheed SR-71 "Blackbird". The SR-71A is the operational reconnaissance variant. The SR-71B is the two-seat trainer. [W]
- **Nicknames:** "Blackbird" and "Habu". [W]
- **Maker:** Lockheed Corporation, Skunk Works. Designer: Clarence "Kelly" Johnson. [W]
- **First flight:** 22 December 1964. [W]
- **Built:** 32 airframes. [W]
- **Era and retirement:** Cold War. USAF retired it in 1989 (temporarily) and finally in 1998. NASA flew it until 1999. [W]
- **Role:** Long-range, high-altitude, Mach 3+ strategic reconnaissance. [W]
- **Crew:** Two in tandem cockpits: a pilot and a Reconnaissance Systems Officer (RSO). [W]
- **Unarmed.** It carried no weapons, only cameras and sensors. (See section 5.)

### Kid-friendly voice lines (each under 12 words, all true)
1. "The Blackbird flew faster than a rifle bullet!" (Top speed about 3,500 km/h, faster than a bullet's roughly 900 m/s. Speed sourced in section 2.)
2. "It flew so high, it was near the edge of space!" (Operating altitude about 85,000 ft, about 26 km. [W])
3. "It was painted black to stay cool when flying fast." (Source: [W] says the black paint radiates heat and reduces skin temperature.)
4. "Its engines swallowed air like a giant vacuum!" (Describes the intake. Engine air-flow claim is a simplification; stated as a kid's paraphrase.)
5. "Its spy cameras could see a car from very high up!" (Cameras were reconnaissance imagers. Specific ground resolution is not sourced here. Keep this line only after confirming it.)

Note on line 5: the resolution claim is not verified. Replace it if unsure.

Consistency check: AR = b²/S = 16.94² / 167.2 = 1.72, matching the stated 1.7.

Variable states: none. The wing and fuselage are fixed. The only moving body surfaces are the engine inlet spikes (inlet spike translation, section 2c) and the nozzle flaps. There are no swept/unswept or tilt states.

### 2b. Fuselage
- **Nose:** Long, slender, pointed. Sharp chines run aft from the nose on both sides. [W] The nose tip is a point with a small radius (est.).
- **Chines:** Sharp, thin edges running from the nose back along the fuselage sides and blending into the wing. [W, MF: "remarkably thin and sharp"] Chine dimensions were not sourced. Estimated: chines start at about 0.10 of length (est.) and widen to a near-full body width at the wing root (est.). Suggest modeling them as a thin sharp fillet edge, not a wide wing.
- **Cross-section:** Roughly circular-to-elliptical fuselage with a flattened underside (est.). Widest point near the wing root (est. about 2.8 m width, see stations). Chines give a sharp corner on the upper sides.
- **Canopy (cockpit):** Two tandem cockpits under a single canopy/windscreen. Canopy dimensions were NOT sourced. Estimated: canopy from about 0.25 to 0.45 of length (est.), with a smooth bubble shape, and the windscreen is a three-layer glass unit. [W]
- **Canopy temperatures [W, MF]:** Outer windscreen can reach about 316 °C (600 °F). Inside the windscreen about 121 °C (250 °F).

### 2c. Intake (engine air inlet)
- **Type:** Mixed external/internal compression inlet with a movable centre-body spike. [W]
- **Position:** Each inlet sits at the front of an engine nacelle under the wing, outboard of the fuselage (est.).
- **Spike:** A translating conical spike that moves aft to control shock position. Maximum rearward travel about 660 mm (26 in), retracting aft above Mach 1.6. [MF]
- **Spike travel rate [TH snippet]:** about 1 5/8 in per 0.1 Mach.
- **Spike cone length:** not sourced. Estimated: spike extends from the inlet lip, length on the order of the nacelle diameter (est.).
- **Boundary layer bleed slots and bypass doors:** The inlet has bleed slots and bypass doors. [W] These are small details; suggest slotted lines or dark strips.

### 2d. Wings
- **Planform:** Very low-aspect, highly swept delta wing with a blended body (lifting body style). [MF calls it a "very low-aspect delta"]. The wing is blended into the chines.
- **Aspect ratio:** 1.7. [MF]
- **Root chord:** not sourced. Estimated from the sourced area and span: for a near-triangular delta, root chord about 20 m (est.). Pick root chord between 18 and 21 m (est.).
- **Tip chord:** not sourced. Estimated small, around 1.5 to 2.5 m (est.), because the trailing edge is notched.
- **Leading-edge sweep:** not sourced (search found no value). Estimated 60 to 72 degrees (est.). Use 65 degrees as the default if no better value is found.
- **Trailing-edge sweep:** not sourced. Trailing edge is roughly straight and inward-angled (est.). Suggest a notched outboard trailing edge.
- **Position:** Wing root leading edge around 0.50 of length (est.). Wing extends back to the rear of the fuselage (est.).
- **Mount:** Low mid-body mount, close to the fuselage underside (est.).
- **Dihedral/anhedral:** not sourced. Assume flat, 0 degrees (est.).
- **Thickness:** not sourced. Estimate t/c about 3 to 4 percent at root (est.).
- **Leading-edge camber:** [W] states the wing uses leading-edge camber to reduce induced drag. Model as a slight droop on the front edge (est.).

### 2e. Tails (vertical fins)
- **Count:** Two vertical fins. [W, MF]
- **Cant:** The fins are canted inward, not vertical. [search snippets; MF mentions "inward-canted fins"] The cant angle was not sourced. Estimate 10 to 15 degrees inward (est.).
- **Sweep:** not sourced. Estimated highly swept, similar to the wing leading edge (est.).
- **Size:** not sourced. Estimate fin height about 3.0 to 3.5 m above the body, with tips reaching the top of the 5.64 m overall height (est.).
- **Horizontal stabilizers:** none. The wing and body serve as the lifting surfaces. (The SR-71 has no horizontal tail; it is a delta with twin fins.)
- **Trainer SR-71B:** [MF] describes "two fixed ventral fins and a fin under each nacelle" on the trainer. The operational SR-71A has the twin vertical fins described above. Note this if the SR-71B is ever modeled.

### 2f. Engines
- **Count:** 2 Pratt & Whitney J58 (JT11D-20J or JT11D-20K). [W]
- **Position:** One nacelle under each wing, outboard of the fuselage, with the inlet at the front (est.). Nacelles extend beyond the wing trailing edge.
- **Engine size [MF]:** length 4.57 m (180 in), diameter 1.27 m (50 in).
- **Nozzle:** Variable exhaust nozzle with blow-in doors and trailing flaps. Flaps fully open at Mach 2.4. [W] Nozzle diameter not sourced. Estimate exhaust opening about 1.1 to 1.2 m (est.).
- **Nozzle plug:** The centre plug in the nozzle was not sourced. Model as a conical plug inside the nozzle (est.).

### 2g. Propellers
None. Jet aircraft.

### 2h. Landing gear [MF]
- **Wheel track:** 5.08 m (16 ft 8 in)
- **Wheelbase:** 11.53 m (37 ft 10 in)
- **Type:** Tricycle gear. Nose gear is a single-wheel unit under the nose (est.). Main gear retracts into the nacelles/fuselage underside (est.).

## 3. Distinctive features checklist (ordered by visual importance)

1. **Long, flat, black delta silhouette.** Very low aspect ratio triangular wing blended into body. (W, MF)
2. **Sharp chines along the nose and fuselage sides.** Thin lines running from the nose back into the wing. (W, MF)
3. **Two inward-canted vertical fins at the tail.** No horizontal tail. (W, MF)
4. **Long black nacelles under the wing with a visible intake spike.** The spike is the single most recognizable detail at the front of each nacelle. (W, MF)
5. **Tandem canopy with a long, three-layer windscreen** set near the nose. (W)
6. **Matte black finish with no markings** on most airframes. Only tail numbers and small stencils. (W)
7. **Long, slim engines extending well behind the wing** with a blown-open exhaust nozzle. (MF)
8. **Small, tall-looking proportion**: overall height is small compared with the 32.74 m length. (W, MF)
9. **No weapons, no guns, no external stores.** The silhouette is clean. (W)
10. **Tall, thin, sharp nose** that curves to a point. (W)

## 4. Paint and liveries

### 4a. Colour facts
- Finish is black. It was chosen to radiate heat, lower skin temperature, and reduce thermal stress. [W]
- The paint is described as a "radar-absorbing deep blue-black that also radiated heat away." [MF]
- Forum and secondary sources describe the paint as a very flat black with iron-based (ferrite) particles, and say it faded to dark grey with flight hours. These are secondary claims; treat them as unverified.
- Wikipedia's specifications section did not include the iron ore paint description in the fetched text.
- **Tail numbers and stencils:** small white or light tail numbers on the fins are part of the standard black scheme (est.). Not sourced for every airframe.

## 6. Engines and propulsion

### 6a. Engine facts
- **Engine:** Pratt & Whitney J58 (JT11D-20J / JT11D-20K). Single-spool turbojet with afterburner. [W, TH]
- **Count:** 2. [W]
- **Thrust, afterburner (wet):** 34,000 lbf (151 kN) for JT11D-20K; 32,500 lbf (145 kN) for JT11D-20J. [W]
- **Thrust, dry (per engine):** 25,000 lbf (110 kN). [W]
- **Thrust, uninstalled at high speed (per wiki overview):** 34,000 lbf (150 kN). [W overview]
- **Installed thrust at zero airspeed:** 25,500 lbf (113 kN). [W overview]
- **Thrust split [TH]:** At Mach 3+, the engine supplies only about one fifth of the propulsion. Most comes from intake compression.
- **Bleed system [TH]:** Compressor air is routed around the engine. Some call it a turboramjet. Bleed air mixes into the afterburner area and is used for thrust and cooling.
- **Afterburner use:** Used for long periods, not just briefly. [TH]
- **Fuel:** JP-7 (special high-flash-point jet fuel). [W]
- **Ignition aid [TH]:** Each engine had a 600 cc tank of triethylborane (TEB), which is pyrophoric and ignites JP-7 on contact with air.

### 6b. Afterburner look
- **Afterburner yes/no:** Yes. [W]
- **Flame:** A long, bright, blue-white flame at high power. (est. description)
- **Shock diamonds:** A chain of diamond-shaped shock cells in the plume. [TH snippet] One eyewitness account describes them about every two feet. Estimated as a repeating diamond pattern (est.).
- **Nozzle colour and material:** The exhaust is titanium and heat-resistant alloy. Not directly sourced. Estimate dark metal with a bluish heat glow (est.).

### 6c. Engine mod ladder (5 steps)
1. **Realistic first: J58 Dry.** Plain dark nozzle, no flame, dry thrust.
2. **Rung 1: J58 Wet.** Afterburner on with a short orange-blue flame.
3. **Rung 2: Shock Diamonds.** Visible diamond-shaped shock cells in the plume.
4. **Rung 3: Titan Blast.** Long bright plume with a faint heat shimmer.
5. **Legendary: Sun Engine.** Golden plume with sparks. Fantasy only.

## 8. Sound (for Web Audio synthesis)

- **Engine type:** Two turbojets with afterburners. No propeller.
- **Character:** Deep, rising turbine roar at takeoff, with a high whine at low power. (est. description)
- **Pitch:** Low fundamental around 90 to 140 Hz (est.) for the fan/compressor whine, rising to a roar in the 200 to 600 Hz range (est.) at high power.
- **Afterburner:** A sharp, broadband roar with a crackle at ignition. Use filtered noise with a rising low-pass cutoff (est.) plus a sharp attack.
- **Idle:** A low hiss with a faint tonal whine.
- **Synthesis suggestion:** Two detuned sawtooth oscillators through a low-pass filter for the turbine tone; white noise through a band-pass filter for the intake and jet roar; a fast envelope for afterburner ignition. All parameters estimated (est.).
