# Glossary

**Game Mode**: One kind of money practice. There are three: Learn the Coins (names and values), Count the Cash (how much is here?) and Pay the Shop (pick the right money). They open in that order: each one after the previous reaches Level 2. The player picks which open Game Mode to play.
_Avoid_: Pay the Garage (it clashes with the Garage, which uses Bolts, not money)

**Level**: How hard a Game Mode is. Levels 1 to 3 are set by which money appears: Level 1 uses pennies, nickels and dimes, Level 2 adds quarters and Level 3 adds $1 and $5 bills. Levels 4 and 5 use all the money and ask for something harder: Learn the Coins asks which one piece is worth the same as a pile (piles of one kind, then mixed piles), Count the Cash counts bigger jumbled piles up to $9.99 and then the change from paying for something, and Pay the Shop pays prices up to $9.75 with the $5 bill and then pays with the fewest pieces. Each Game Mode has its own Level. The game raises it itself when the player shows mastery; nobody picks it by hand, and it never goes down.

**Stretch**: How big a problem is within its Level: smaller piles, lower prices and fewer answers to pick from while the player is new to the Level or missing a lot, growing as their first-try answers outnumber their misses.
_Avoid_: Level (a Stretch never changes the money or the kind of problem)

**Mastery**: Getting 8 of the last 10 problems in a Game Mode right on the first try without help, or the last 6 in a row. It raises that Game Mode's Level; at Level 5 it gives the star. Only problems asked at the player's Level count.

**Round**: A short run of problems in one Game Mode that ends in a reward. A problem he missed comes back once, later in the Round. Above Level 1 the first problem, and the one right after a miss or help, is a warm-up from the Level below that doesn't count toward Mastery.

**Bolt**: What the player earns in Rounds, one for each answer right on the first try without help plus one for finishing, and spends to unlock Mods and Colours and buy Bodies. It is deliberately *not* money, so spending it never asks the player to count cash. A Bolt never buys a Legendary Mod.
_Avoid_: Reward Token, coin, star, point

**Truck**: The player's one monster truck: a Body with Mods fitted to it. A player has one Truck at a time.

**Body**: The basic shape of the Truck. There are starter Bodies, which the player picks from on first launch and owns from the start, and Bodies bought with Bolts. The player can switch between owned Bodies at any time, and each Body remembers which Mods were fitted to it.
_Avoid_: Starter Truck, model

**Slot**: One place on the Truck that holds a single Mod at a time, such as Tires or Paint.
_Avoid_: Category, part type

**Mod**: A part, look or sound that goes in one Slot (tires, paint, flames, lights, an engine and so on). A Mod is unlocked once, by spending Bolts (or money, for a Legendary), and cooler Mods cost more. After that it can be fitted to any Body or taken off freely.

**Rung**: A Mod's step within its Slot: first, second, top, then Legendary. A higher Rung looks cooler and costs more. The first three are paid in Bolts; the Legendary Rung is paid in money, tapped into the Pay the Shop tray to the exact price, and opens for purchase once the top Rung in its Slot is unlocked. A Legendary's price follows the player's Pay the Shop Level (Levels 4 and 5 keep Level 3's prices) and uses only that Level's money, so it grows as he improves; no Level is needed to buy one. Each Slot also has a free default that is not a Rung.
_Avoid_: Tier, level (Level means how hard a Game Mode is)

**Door Number**: The racing number on the Truck's door, 0 to 99, which the player picks himself with two wheels (tens and ones) while it is read aloud. Each Body keeps its own number, and changing it is always free. It is not a Mod: the Door Number Slot's Mods are only its style (Plain, Outlined, Flaming, Glowing Gold), and with No Number fitted it isn't shown.

**Colour**: The base coat of one Body, such as Red, Teal or Sky Blue. There are twelve. Red is free and the rest cost 3 Bolts each, bought once and then used on any Body. Each Body keeps its own Colour. It is not a Mod and not a Slot: it sits alongside the Paint Slot and is picked from a row of swatches in the Paint sheet. It shows on Plain and Fire Fade (the fire fades into it); the other Paint Mods cover it.
_Avoid_: paint (Paint is the Slot), color

**Goal**: The one locked Mod, Body or Colour the player is saving Bolts toward, shown as a progress bar while they play. It is the cheapest Mod or Body unless the player taps another locked Mod, Body or Colour to choose it. A Colour becomes the Goal on its own only once nothing else bought with Bolts is left. A Legendary is never the Goal, because it is bought with money.

**Garage**: Where the Truck is built. It shows the Truck, the Mods already unlocked and the Mods still to unlock, each with its Bolt cost (or money price, for a Legendary), and the Bodies owned and still to buy.

**Show Off**: The Garage's stage moment: the current Truck spins under a spotlight while the crowd cheers, and performs a move when tapped.

**Trophy**: A reward earned by playing well: reaching a Level or a star, a streak of answers right on the first try, a perfect Round, or sticking with it (Rounds finished, days played). A Trophy is never bought and never lost. It isn't a Mod, it isn't bought with Bolts and it earns none. A counting Trophy upgrades in place, bronze to silver to gold and on, rather than taking a new spot. Trophies stand on the Trophy Shelf in the Garage, on three planks: Learning, Skill and Sticking with it. Earned ones shine and the rest are grey silhouettes.
_Avoid_: badge, achievement, award

## Time Flies

Time Flies reuses the terms above where they mean the same thing (Round, Level, Mastery, Bolt, Slot, Mod, Rung, Goal,
Trophy). These are its own:

**Game Modes (Time Flies)**: the six kinds of question. The clock: Find the Clock (hear a time, tap the clock that shows
it), Read the Clock (one clock, pick its time from three read aloud) and Set the Clock (drag the hands); Level 1 is
whole hours, Level 2 quarter hours and Level 3 every five minutes. The calendar: Days of the Week (tomorrow, then
yesterday, then two days on), Months of the Year (the month after, the month before, then "the third month") and Time
Facts (how many seconds in a minute, minutes in an hour, hours in a day...). Find the Clock, Days of the Week and Time
Facts are open from the start; Read the Clock opens when Find the Clock reaches Level 2, Set the Clock after Read the
Clock, and Months of the Year after Days of the Week. He never picks one: every Round mixes all the open ones.

**Mixed Round (Time Flies)**: each question is a different kind from the one before. Most are asked at that kind's
Level, to push him, and only those count toward Mastery; about one in three, and always the one right after a miss,
comes from an easier Level.

**Mastery (Time Flies)**: Getting 16 of the last 20 questions of one kind, asked at its Level, right on the first try
without help, twice the run Monster Truck Money asks for, so he stays on each Level longer.

**Aircraft**: one of the 18 planes in the Hangar, Time Flies' Body. Three starters (P-51 Mustang, F-16 Falcon, F/A-18
Super Hornet) are his from the start; the rest cost 30 Bolts.
_Avoid_: jet (the P-51, P-38 and Corsair have propellers), Body

**Hangar**: where he builds his Aircraft, Time Flies' Garage.

**Time Lock**: the vault that holds every Legendary in Time Flies. Once a Slot's top Rung is his, tapping its Legendary
asks him to set the clock to a time; the vault opens when he does. A Legendary is never bought with Bolts.

**Take Off**: the Hangar's stage moment, Time Flies' Show Off: the Aircraft rolls down a runway, lifts off and climbs
into the sky with its Smoke and Engine. Up there it does a trick when tapped, fires its guns while 🔫 is held, and each 🚀
tap launches the next store its Weapons Mod hangs under the wings (reloaded a few seconds later). 🛬 lands it back on
the runway and returns to the Hangar.

**Tail Number**: Time Flies' Door Number, painted on the fins.

## Chop Chop

Chop Chop reuses Time Flies' terms where they mean the same thing (Mixed Round, Mastery, Bolt, Slot, Mod, Rung, Goal,
Trophy, Tail Number). These are its own:

**Game Modes (Chop Chop)**: the seven kinds of fraction question: Fair Shares, Name the Piece, Bigger Piece, Find the
Part, Color the Part, Share It and Top and Bottom. Fair Shares, Find the Part and Share It are open from the start; Name
the Piece opens when Fair Shares reaches Level 2, Bigger Piece and Top and Bottom after Name the Piece, and Color the Part
after Find the Part. Mastery is 16 of the last 20, or 10 in a row.

**Part**: some of the equal pieces of a whole, said in words ("three quarters", "three of the four equal pieces"), and,
once he has met writing, also written as numbers.

**Top and Bottom**: a part written with numbers, the top number over the bottom number (3 over 4). The bottom number is
"how many equal pieces" and the top number is "how many pieces are left". It's always read aloud with the words he knows
("three quarters"), never as "three over four" or "three fourths". Writing is met once, before the first Top and Bottom
question; after that the numbers are shown beside the words everywhere.
_Avoid_: numerator, denominator (to him; fine in code comments)

**Helicopter**: one of the 17 on the Helipad, Chop Chop's Body. Three starters (UH-60 Black Hawk, MH-6 Little Bird,
AH-1Z Viper); the rest cost 30 Bolts.

**Helipad**: where he builds his Helicopter, Chop Chop's Garage.

**Fuel Lock**: the vault that holds every Legendary in Chop Chop. Once a Slot's top Rung is his, tapping its Legendary
asks him to fill the fuel tank to a part ("three quarters full"); it opens when he does.

**Lift Off**: the Helipad's stage moment: the rotors spin up, it lifts straight off the helipad and flies into the sky,
does a trick when tapped, fires its gun and rockets, and lands back on the pad.

## Sight Squad

Sight Squad reuses Chop Chop's terms where they mean the same thing (Mixed Round, Mastery, Slot, Mod, Rung, Goal,
Trophy). These are its own:

**Ammo**: Sight Squad's Bolt, drawn as a brass bullet, earned and spent exactly as Bolts are, because an army runs on
ammunition, not machine parts. Every game names its currency in `src/currency/kind.ts`.
_Avoid_: Bolt (in Sight Squad it's always Ammo)

**Sight word**: one of the 100 common words he learns (Dolch pre-primer and primer, Fry's first hundred), met one at a
time in a fixed order of 20 sets of 5. Look-alikes (was/saw, on/no, the/then) are never in the same set.
_Avoid_: vocabulary word, spelling word

**Heart word**: a sight word with letters that don't say what they usually say (the *ai* in *said*), its **heart part**.
Those letters get a heart when it's taught, to be learned "by heart"; the rest of the word is read the usual way.

**Meeting a word**: the moment a new word arrives, before any question uses it: it's shown big, said, spelled letter by
letter ("Say it, spell it, say it"), its heart part shown, used in a sentence, and then he taps it.

**Strength**: how well he knows one word, 0 to 5: up one on a first-try answer, down two on a miss. Weak and new words
come up more often. The next word waits while more than two are shaky (below 2).
_Avoid_: Level (a Level belongs to a kind of question, not a word)

**Game Modes (Sight Squad)**: the six kinds of question: Find It (hear a word, tap it), Word Hunt (tap every copy in a
grid), Build It (tap its letters in order), Read It (read a word, pick which of three spoken words it is), Sentence Hunt
(find the word in a sentence read to him) and Fill the Gap (pick the missing word of a sentence). Find It, Word Hunt and
Build It are open from the start; each opens the one below it on Home at Level 2.

**Army**: his toy soldiers, green plastic army men on a play-mat, Sight Squad's Body. It's many figures, not one.

**Squad**: one kind of figure in the Army (Rifleman, Scout, Drummer, Medic, Rocket Trooper, Army Dog, Paratrooper, Jeep,
Cannon, Tank). Riflemen are his from the start; the rest are unlocked once with Ammo, and the first soldier comes with it.
_Avoid_: unit type, class

**Recruit**: one more soldier of a Squad he has, bought with Ammo (2 to 12) and bought again as often as he likes, up to
the Squad's maximum. It marches onto the mat. Recruits are never the Goal.

**Gear**: the Mods the whole Army wears, one Rung per Slot: Helmet, Pack, Flag, Finish, Mat (the play-mat itself) and Boom
(what it throws in a Battle).

**Team Colour**: the one colour of plastic the whole Army is made of. Army Green, Tan and Grey are free (he picks one on
first launch); nine more cost 3 Ammo.
_Avoid_: paint, Colour (in Sight Squad it's always the Team Colour)

**Camp**: where he builds his Army, Sight Squad's Garage.

**Password Lock**: the vault that holds every Legendary in Sight Squad. Once a Slot's top Rung is his, tapping its
Legendary asks him to build a password, a word he has met, from letter tiles.

**Parade**: one of the Camp's two stage moments: the Army marches round the mat in a column, and does what the command words
he taps say (go, stop, run, jump, look, come, down, up, play: only the ones he has met, written only).

**Battle**: the other stage moment: a toy battle against wind-up robots. Robots that are hit topple over and bounce back up,
then go poof; his soldiers that are hit fall over and spring back up. He always wins. Never "kill", "shoot" or "destroy".

## Math Bots

Math Bots reuses Sight Squad's terms where they mean the same thing (Mixed Round, Mastery, Stretch, Bolt, Strength, Meeting, Goal). These are its own:

**Game Modes (Math Bots)**: the thirteen kinds of question: Make Ten, Count On, Bot Stories, Facts, Tens and Ones, Missing Part, Skip Count, Number Chart, Measure It, Shapes, Weigh-In, Over Ten and Big Jumps. Make Ten, Count On and Bot Stories are open from the start; `src/math-bots/learning.ts` says what opens the rest. Over Ten needs two parents, Make Ten at Level 3 and Tens and Ones at Level 2, and so does Big Jumps, Tens and Ones at Level 3 and Number Chart at Level 2.

**Working set**: the kinds a Round practises: at most five that have no star yet, lowest Level first. Starred kinds come back as about one review question a Round.

**Tune-Up**: his first Rounds, which look like any other but probe five anchors, three questions each. Three clean answers start that kind at Level 2; two misses stop the row. Nobody sets a Level by hand. It places low, because Levels never go down, and the first 30 questions of a kind asked at his Level use a faster Mastery rule so he climbs quickly.

**Meeting (Math Bots)**: the first time a question needs a picture (ten frame, number path, number bond, a ten, the same-as sign, the less and more signs, number chart, a wrench as a unit, sides of a shape, filling an outline), it's shown and said, and he taps it.

**Fact family**: 3+5, 5+3, 8-3 and 8-5 are one family, with one Strength.

**Counter**: the plain round token in two colours used on every question screen. A Bolt is never used as a counter, and no robot part appears on a question screen.

**Scoreboard**: a three-row chart in Bot Stories, one plain square per hit.

**Fix It**: after he rightly says a Weigh-In isn't the same, he taps counters on or off to even the pans.

**Number Chart (the picture)**: 1 to 120 in rows of ten, so one row down is ten more. A short screen shows only the rows that fit.

**Unit (Math Bots)**: a wrench laid along a bar to measure it. All the same size, end to end, tapped down one at a time; the word on screen is always wrench.

**Outline**: the dashed shape he fills in Shapes Level 3, by tapping the piece that fits. A piece turns itself: there is no rotating.

**Workshop**: the reward screen: his robot on a bench, Power and three bars, the Fight button with the next rival on it, and the tray of parts.

**Part**: anything a robot is built from: a Body, Wheels, something for a Mount, a Paint or a Face. Rivals are built from the same list, and every part that isn't a Paint or a Face changes the fight. Bought with Bolts, won from a rival, or opened in the Balance Lock.
_Avoid_: Mod (Math Bots has parts, not Mods)

**Mount**: one of the four spots a part bolts to: front, right, back, left. A Body has some of them. A part guards or hits only from its own side.

**Power**: the one number that sums what's fitted to a robot, shown beside rivals' so he can see what he's up against.

**TORQUE**: his robot, whatever he builds it into.

**Fight (Math Bots)**: the reward, with no math in it. He drives his robot from above with a stick or by touching the arena, with a 💥 and a 💨 button. Each finished Round earns one. A win uses it; a loss leaves one more go on it. The word rule: knocked out, stopped, sent spinning.

**League**: twelve rivals in a row. He fights the next one or goes back to any he's beaten. Each pays Bolts and one prize part nothing else gives.

**Rival**: a computer-driven robot in the League, with its own way of fighting, and a line of advice said when it beats him. Each loss to one softens it a little until he wins.

**Legendary (Math Bots)**: Rainbow Paint, Lightning Drum, Dragon Armour, Comet Wheels. Never bought with Bolts and never the Goal, and each waits on the dearest part of its sort.

**Balance Lock**: the vault that holds every Legendary in Math Bots. Tapping an open Legendary asks him to make two pans the same, a Weigh-In as hard as his own Level. It costs no Bolts, a miss costs nothing, and it changes no Level.

## Rover Code

Rover Code reuses Math Bots' terms where they mean the same thing (Mixed Round, Mastery, Stretch, Bolt, Tune-Up, Meeting, Goal, Part, Power). These are its own:

**Game Modes (Rover Code)**: the four kinds of question: Drive It (build a program that takes the rover to the flag), Patterns (what comes next), Bug Hunt (find and fix the wrong step) and Loops (a step done again and again). Drive It and Patterns are open from the start; Bug Hunt opens at Drive It Level 2, and Loops once both Drive It and Patterns reach Level 2.

**Program**: the row of arrow steps he taps into the program bar. Each step moves the rover one square up, right, down or left as the screen shows it, never "turn left". Any program that ends on the flag is right.
_Avoid_: code, commands (to him)

**Step**: one arrow in a program.

**Loop**: some steps done a number of times, shown as a 🔁 block with ×3. Rover Code's own word for what code calls repeat.
_Avoid_: repeat block, for-loop

**Bug**: the one wrong step in a Bug Hunt program. A Bug Hunt is only made when exactly one step can be changed to fix it.

**Space Base**: where he builds his rocket, Rover Code's Garage: the launch pad, the planet map, the Launch button and the parts tray.

**Rocket**: his rocket, Rover Code's Body, built from four slots (Nose, Body, Fins, Engine) and a Paint. Its Power (4 to 28) decides which planets it can reach.

**Launch**: one trip, earned by finishing a Round (he starts with one). It's used only when he plants his flag.
_Avoid_: fuel, ticket

**Trip**: the reward, with no puzzle in it: the launch countdown, the flight (steer with a finger, catch Bolts, dodge asteroids) and the planet drive (drive the rover, jump for crystals, plant the flag).

**Planet**: one of sixteen in a row (Moon, Mars, Frosty, Ringo, Lava World, Crystal World, Jungle World, Rainbow World, Candy World, Bubble World, Ocean World, Cactus World, Mushroom World, Thunder World, Robot World, Star World), each needing more Power.

**Rover**: the rover he drives on planets. He starts with the yellow Scout Rover (also the one on the question grid) and can buy five more kinds at the Space Base (Dune Buggy, Monster Truck, Tank Crawler, Spider Walker, Hover Rover), each with its own Trick. He drives one at a time.

**Trick**: what a rover or a Kit gadget does to the drive, said when he taps it: "It goes extra fast!", "It jumps extra high!", "It never slips on ice!", "It can jump in the air!", "It floats!".

**Gadget**: a thing a rover wears. Sixteen are won, one on the first visit to each planet; eight more (the Kit) are bought. Each rover has its own set switched on, and a gadget is on unless he has switched it off on that rover. Some change the drive (Big Wheels, Solar Wings, Jump Booster, Crystal Magnet, Confetti Cannon, Turbo Jets, Robot Arm, Snow Plow, Bubble Blower, Parachute, Fireworks).
_Avoid_: Mod, feature (to him)

**Kit**: the gadgets bought with Bolts rather than won on a planet: Spoiler, Flame Decals, Snow Plow, Bubble Blower, Parachute, Disco Ball, Fireworks, Alien Pal.

**Crystal**: what he finds on a planet, five per trip, one Bolt each.

## Treasure Fleet

Treasure Fleet reuses Math Bots' and Rover Code's terms where they mean the same thing (Mixed Round, Mastery, Stretch, Meeting, Goal, Part, Rung). It has no Tune-Up. These are its own:

**Doubloon**: Treasure Fleet's Bolt, drawn as a gold pirate coin, earned and spent exactly as Bolts are, because pirates
sail for gold, not machine parts. Every game names its currency in `src/currency/kind.ts`.
_Avoid_: Bolt, coin, gold piece (in Treasure Fleet it's always Doubloons)

**Game Modes (Treasure Fleet)**: the five kinds of question, all asked on an island map: Where Is It (tap the thing in a position or direction from another), Map Key (what the symbols mean), Grid Square (name a square by its letter and number), Follow the Clues (spoken directions from a star to the treasure) and From Above (which map shows a scene seen from the side). Where Is It and Map Key are open from the start; Grid Square opens at Where Is It Level 2, From Above at Map Key Level 2, and Follow the Clues once both Where Is It and Map Key reach Level 2.

**Map**: the parchment island a question is asked on, in squares, with things standing on some of them. North is always up. Left and right are the screen's, never a character's.

**Symbol**: one of the eight things drawn on maps, the map key and the sea chart: palm tree, hut, cave, hill, rock, dock, bridge (only on a river) and river.
_Avoid_: icon, picture (to him)

**Map Key**: the panel beside a map that shows each symbol with its word. Also the name of the kind that teaches it.
_Avoid_: legend

**Compass Rose**: the N E S W star in a map's corner. He meets it once, with the rhyme "Never Eat Soggy Waffles", before the first question that uses it.

**Grid Square**: a square named by a letter along the bottom and a number up the side, letter first (B3). Each column letter has its own colour.

**Clue**: one spoken direction in Follow the Clues: "Go north 2 squares", or at Level 3 a landmark, "Go east to the hut".

**Shipyard**: where he builds his ship, Treasure Fleet's Garage: the dock, the sea chart, the Set Sail button and the parts tray.

**Ship**: his ship, Treasure Fleet's Body, built from four slots (Hull, Sails, Cannons, Figurehead) and a Flag (an emblem he buys and colours he picks for free). It starts as a rowing boat with a bedsheet sail and ends as a ghost ship. Its Range (4 to 28) decides which islands it can reach.

**Range**: how far the ship can sail, Treasure Fleet's Power: one for each slot's Rung plus one.

**Voyage**: one trip, earned by finishing a Round (he starts with one): sailing to an island (the sea, from above), then the island (from the side) and the dig. It's used only when the chest opens.
_Avoid_: launch, ticket

**Sea Chart**: the map of the islands in the Shipyard, with a compass rose and a key; tapping an island in reach makes it the next stop. It is never a question.

**Island**: one of sixteen (Turtle Island, Palm Island, Parrot Island, Skull Rock, Mermaid Lagoon, Volcano Island, Kraken Reef, Starfall Island, Seashell Cove, Iceberg Island, Banana Island, Lighthouse Point, Shipwreck Bay, Thunder Isle, Ghost Island, Rainbow Reef), each needing more Range and each hiding one Treasure.

**Treasure**: the named prize in an island's chest the first time he digs there (the Golden Compass, the Ruby Skull, the Kraken's Pearl...). Every chest also holds gold (Doubloons).
_Avoid_: Trophy (Trophies are earned by playing well; Treasures by sailing)

**Hoard**: the cave where every Treasure he has found is shown, with his ship moored outside.

**Barrel**: what floats by at sea, three a Voyage, one Doubloon each.

## Wild Rescue

Wild Rescue reuses Math Bots', Rover Code's and Treasure Fleet's terms where they mean the same thing (Mixed Round, Mastery, Stretch, Goal, Part, Rung, Paint). It has no Tune-Up and no Meetings. These are its own:

**Paw Print**: Wild Rescue's Bolt, drawn as an orange paw, earned and spent exactly as Bolts are, because it's an animal's thank-you, not a machine part. The drive to a Rescue has four to pick up. Every game names its currency in `src/currency/kind.ts`.
_Avoid_: Bolt (in Wild Rescue it's always Paw Prints)

**Game Modes (Wild Rescue)**: the five kinds of question, about first-grade science: Where It Lives (an animal's habitat), Animal Parts (what a body part is for), Alive or Not (living things and what they need), Babies (young animals and their parents) and Sky and Seasons (day and night, the weather, the seasons, the sun across the day). Where It Lives and Animal Parts are open from the start; Alive or Not and Sky and Seasons open at Where It Lives Level 2, Babies at Animal Parts Level 2.

**Facts table**: the hand-checked data every question is made from (`src/wild-rescue/facts/`): the animals, the body parts, living things and needs, the babies, and the sky and seasons. A question can only offer what the table says is plainly right or plainly wrong.
_Avoid_: question bank

**Habitat**: one of eight places an animal lives: ocean, pond and river, forest, rainforest, grassland, desert, polar, farm. Only an animal with one clear habitat is asked about or lives in the Park. Also the Park's plots for them (the farm's is the Barn, the ocean's the Ocean Pool, the polar one Polar Ice).
_Avoid_: biome, home (to him, "its home" is fine in speech)

**Did You Know**: the one short true line each animal carries, said when it comes home to the Park, when he taps it there, and in a fuller lesson.

**Rescue call**: what a finished Round earns (he starts with one): the radio names an animal in trouble ("A sea turtle is caught in a fishing net!"). It's used only when the animal is in the crate.
_Avoid_: voyage, launch, ticket

**Rescue**: answering a call: the drive (from the side, hold to drive, tap to jump), the Job, and the ride home with the animal in the crate.

**Job**: the hands-on part at the end of a Rescue, one of three: pull the net off, lift it out with the winch, or lead it to the crate with treats. It can't be failed.

**Release**: a call once every animal his truck can reach is already home: the same drive and Job, and then the animal goes back to the wild. It pays the same and the Park keeps what it has.

**Truck**: his rescue truck, Wild Rescue's Body, built from four slots (Tires, Winch, Lights, Carrier), a Buddy who rides in the cab window, and a Paint. Some parts open rescues: Mud Tires the forest and the rainforest, Sand Tires the desert, Snow Tracks the polar lands, the Boat Trailer the ponds, the Water Tank the ocean, the Big Crate large animals and the Giant Trailer the biggest.

**Garage**: where he builds the truck (Wild Rescue reuses the word for its own truck).

**Park**: the map of habitat plots he buys with Paw Prints, where his rescued animals live, with Extras inside the habitats and Decorations on the path. He carries each new animal to its habitat there.

**Extra**: one of five things for looks inside each habitat (Hay Bales, Lily Pads, Northern Lights, Banana Tree...).

**Decoration**: a thing for the Park's path (Flower Beds, Fountain, Park Train, Ferris Wheel...).

**Buddy**: a friend who rides along in the truck's cab window (Puppy to Robot), for looks only.


## Sentence Express

Sentence Express reuses Math Bots', Rover Code's and Wild Rescue's terms where they mean the same thing (Mixed Round, Mastery, Stretch, Goal, Paint). It has no Tune-Up and no Meetings. These are its own:

**Ticket**: Sentence Express's Bolt, drawn as a train ticket, earned and spent exactly as Bolts are, because a train set runs
on tickets, not machine parts. Every game names its currency in `src/currency/kind.ts`.
_Avoid_: Bolt (in Sentence Express it's always Tickets)

**Game Modes (Sentence Express)**: the five kinds of question, about reading sentences: Build the Train (put a heard sentence's word cars in order), Engine and Caboose (the capital at the start, the mark at the end, the capital on "I" and names), What Happened (read a sentence alone and tap its picture), Who, What, Where (a question about a sentence) and Fix the Train (find the one mistake and fix it). Build the Train and Engine and Caboose are open from the start; What Happened opens at Build the Train Level 2, Who, What, Where at What Happened Level 2, Fix the Train at Engine and Caboose Level 2.

**Sentence train**: the picture every kind uses: a sentence is a train. The **engine** carries the first word and its capital; each word after it is a **car** (a word car); the **caboose** carries the end mark (. ? !). In the questions the engine is his own engine in his own paint.
_Avoid_: word tiles, blocks

**One right order**: the rule for Build the Train and Fix the Train: no other order of a sentence's cars, with the engine first and the caboose last, makes a sentence that is grammatical and sensible. Checked by the order checker (`src/sentence-express/sentences/grammar.ts`) over every sentence.

**Yard**: where a Build the Train question's cars wait, shuffled, before he couples them onto his train.

**Train Set**: Sentence Express's reward: his engines and cars (the Shed), his Layout, and the Deliveries he drives on it.

**Shed**: where he buys and chooses his engine, the cars coupled behind it (up to six), his paint, track, scenery and sky (Sentence Express's Garage). A long tab is split into **groups** (Freight Cars, Passenger Cars, Trick Cars; Colors, Patterns; Track Boxes, Tables, Track Colors; Nature, Town, Fun, On the Track, Working Toys), picked with a row of emoji chips.
_Avoid_: garage, depot

**Layout**: his table of squares, where he lays track with a finger, rubs it out and puts scenery; it's saved and is the thing he shows off. Straights, corners, points and crossings are worked out from what he draws; a loose end closes up by itself.
_Avoid_: map, board

**Track Box**: a box of 12 track squares for his Layout (2 to 5 Tickets). He starts with 40 squares; a bigger table is bought on the same Track tab.

**Track Color**: the colour all his track is drawn in (Wood Track is free; Rainbow Track changes colour every square; Glow Track and Lava Track glow at night). Bought on the Track tab; one is on at a time.

**Trick**: what his engine does when he taps the trick button while he drives (in a Delivery or Watch it go): every engine has one (a puff of smoke, a boost, sparks, a horn, dragon fire, a siren, a rainbow trail...), with its own sound and word. A **trick car** does its own thing at the same time (dumps its load, throws confetti, lets balloons go...) and again when it brings the cargo home.
_Avoid_: power, ability, special

**Working Toy**: a piece of scenery that moves: crossing gates and a signal that work as the train comes, a drawbridge he taps to let a boat through (it always comes down for the train), a car wash and a rainbow arch the train goes through, windmills and a big wheel that turn, and pieces that go off when tapped while the train runs (a volcano, a launch pad, fireworks...). Any other piece hops when tapped.

**Sky**: the Shed's tab for the time of day (Sunny Day, Sunset, Night Sky) and the weather (snow, rain, a storm, fireworks, northern lights...) on his table while the train runs. At night the stations, lit scenery, the engine's headlight and passenger cars that **light up at night** shine.

**Watch it go**: his train running round his Layout by itself, to show it off: free, any time, from the Layout. He can slide the throttle, use the whistle, bell and trick, switch points, tap his scenery and change the sky. It uses no Delivery and pays nothing.
_Avoid_: free play, demo

**Points**: a square of track that joins three sides: the train goes straight on or takes the branch, and he taps it to switch.
_Avoid_: switch, junction (in anything he hears)

**Delivery**: what a finished Round earns (he starts with one): he drives his train on his Layout to pick up a station's cargo and bring it to Toy Town. It's used, and paid, only when the cargo arrives.
_Avoid_: trip, run, rescue call

**Station**: a stop on his Layout: Toy Town (home) and sixteen on the line (the Farm, the Quarry, the Port, the City, the Mill, the Mine, the Zoo, the Toy Shop, the Bakery, the Beach, the Airport, the Candy Factory, the North Pole, the Dino Park, the Pirate Cove, the Space Center), each with its own cargo. The Farm is open from the start; each finished Delivery opens the next, and a station's first Delivery brings a **gift** piece of scenery.
## Word Wreckers

Word Wreckers reuses Math Bots', Rover Code's, Treasure Fleet's and Wild Rescue's terms where they mean the same thing (Mixed Round, Mastery, Stretch, Bolt, Goal, Paint). It has no Tune-Up. These are its own:

**Game Modes (Word Wreckers)**: the five kinds of spelling question, each about a word he hears and never sees until he has built it: Build It (letter tiles into the sound boxes), Missing Sound (one box empty; pick its tile), Chunks (build it from sound chunks such as sh, ck, ee, a_e), Fix It (a wrong spelling of the word he hears; tap the mistake and fix it) and Which Looks Right (tap the right spelling). Build It and Missing Sound are open from the start; Chunks opens at Build It Level 2, Fix It at Missing Sound Level 2, Which Looks Right at Chunks Level 2.

**Pattern**: one step of the phonics order shared with Dino Dig: short vowels (CVC), digraphs, blends, magic e, vowel teams, r-controlled vowels. Each word on the list (`src/dino-dig/words/list.ts`) belongs to the latest Pattern it uses. A Pattern is met before it is asked, and keeps a strength (0 to 5) so weak ones come back more often. A Level is how hard the question is, not which Pattern it uses.
_Avoid_: word set (that's Sight Squad's)

**Meeting a Pattern**: the short spoken introduction to a new Pattern, inside a Round: what's new ("Two letters can make one sound."), then three example words in their sound boxes with the new sounds lit. It counts as one of the Round's five.

**Sound box**: one box per sound in a word, holding that sound's letters (sh is one box; a magic e sits in its own little box at the end, linked to its vowel's by colour). Lessons light the boxes one at a time while the word is said slowly.
_Avoid_: Elkonin box (fine in the research, not on screen)

**Wrong spelling**: a misspelling the game shows (Fix It) or offers (Which Looks Right). Never a real word, never rude, never read aloud.

**Job**: what a finished Round earns (he starts with one): a demolition and then a build, or a Road Job. It's used only when the new building is up (or the road done).
_Avoid_: rescue, call, voyage

**Road Job**: the second kind of Job, picked by choosing the Steamroller or the Cement Mixer on the Job's card: he drives along an old road to roll its bumps flat or fill its holes, the line gets painted, and he picks a road feature. Pays the same as any Job.

**Road feature**: what a Road Job puts on the Town's road (Car Wash, Jump Ramp, Speed Bumps, Tunnel, Mud Puddle, Loop the Loop, Bubble Arch, Confetti Gate, Sprinklers, Rainbow Arch, Turbo Pad, Firework Pad), three places a street. It works when he drives through it or taps it. Never bought.
_Avoid_: decoration (a decoration is bought and only hops)

**Treasure (Word Wreckers)**: one of 30 named finds (Old Coin, Dino Bone, Time Capsule...) in a chest hidden in every other old building. He taps the chest open; it's kept when the Job is done and sits on the **Treasure shelf** (the Yard's 💎 tab). Never bought.

**Demolition**: the first half of a Job: he knocks an old building down with one of his machines, one of six ways: swing (a wrecking ball he pulls back and lets go), push (a blade he holds to drive in), bite (a bucket that bites where he taps), hammer (a jackhammer he taps fast), blast (dynamite he sticks on, then the **plunger**) or magnet (lifts the top off a column he taps). The old building is wood, brick, concrete or steel, never stronger than his strongest machine, and has a name ("An old brick chimney!").

**Build**: the second half of a Job: he picks one of two or three buildings and lowers its pieces with the crane, moving the hook with his finger and letting go; every piece snaps onto the one below.

**Machine**: one of his 19 construction machines (Wrecking Ball Crane, Mini Digger, Skid Steer, Bulldozer, Jackhammer, Steamroller, Excavator, Cement Mixer, Dump Truck, Dynamite Truck, Magnet Crane, Tower Crane, Rock Breaker, Demolition Robot, Monster Dozer, Super Magnet, Mega Wrecker, Mega Blaster, Giant Excavator). Its **power** (1 wood to 4 steel) is what it can knock down; power **Upgrades** raise it, other upgrades make hits bigger or driving faster, or add looks. The Tower Crane puts up the tallest buildings; the Dump Truck, once his, is driven to haul the rubble; the Steamroller and Cement Mixer do Road Jobs.
_Avoid_: truck, Body (Word Wreckers has no Body or Slots)

**Yard**: where his machines are parked and bought, with their upgrades, paints, the **Driver** (who sits in every cab), his **Horn** (what every machine honks with), the Town's streets, decorations and traffic, and his Treasure shelf.
_Avoid_: Garage

**Town**: the street of everything he has built, lot by lot; he drives any machine along it and honks. Main Street is free; each bought **Street** adds six **Lots** and three places for road features. Six streets are **districts** with their own scenery (Farm Lane, Fairground, Beach Road, Airport Way, Snowy Peak, Dino Valley). With no empty lot, a Job makes one of his buildings **gold** instead.

**Traffic**: vehicles he buys that drive up and down the Town by themselves (Taxi, Tractor, Race Car, Racing Snail...). Tapping one honks it.

**Decoration (Word Wreckers)**: a thing for the Town's pavement or sky (Trees, Fountain, Fire Truck, Ferris Wheel, Fireworks, Flying Dragon...). Tapping one makes it hop and say its name.

## Turbo Tally

Turbo Tally reuses Math Bots' and Rover Code's terms where they mean the same thing (Mixed Round, Mastery, Stretch, Bolt, Tune-Up, Meeting, Goal, Paint), and the Truck's Body, Slot and Mod where his race car has them: a **Body** is the car's shape, a **Slot** one place on it that holds one part (Engine, Tyres, Wing, Booster, Paint, Rims, Decals, Horn, Boost Flame), and its parts are Mods by another name (the game says "parts"). These are its own:

**Game Modes (Turbo Tally)**: the five kinds of question, about comparing, places, tallies and graphs: Podium (places in a line of cars: first to tenth, and what a pass does), Faster Car (which speed number is bigger, then the < > = signs), Tally Board (tally marks in bundles of five), Race Chart (picture and bar graphs of three cars' wins) and Line Up (put three numbers in order; which number goes between two others). Podium and Faster Car are open from the start; Tally Board opens at Podium Level 2, Race Chart and Line Up at Faster Car Level 2. Three Laps (adding three numbers) was dropped because Math Bots' Bot Stories already teaches it.

**Speed, Grip, Boost**: his car's three numbers in the Garage, shown as bars with the number on each. They are real: the Race uses them. The Engine (and a little the Body) sets Speed, the Tyres and Wing (and a little the Body) set Grip, the Booster sets Boost, the number of boosts he gets in a Race.
_Avoid_: power, stats (to him)

**Race**: the reward he drives: three laps of a Track from above, against three Rivals, about a minute. A finished Round earns one (he starts with one). It is used only when he crosses the line; leaving halfway costs nothing. Every finish pays Bolts and is cheered; a Race can't be lost.
_Avoid_: lap (for the whole Race), match

**Star Race (Turbo Tally)**: a Race on a track he has already beaten, for one of its three Stars. Every Rival races at full pace and faster with each Star, and only first place wins it. After three Stars a track is "Just for fun".
_Avoid_: rematch, replay

**Mystery Race (Turbo Tally)**: the endless ladder of Races on tracks made on the spot from a Level number, against five Rivals from every Cup. It opens with the Tarmac Cup. First place goes up a Level, and every fifth Level wins a Mystery part, which is never sold.
_Avoid_: random race, endless mode (to him)

**Track**: one circuit, a closed centre line and a width, on one surface (tarmac, dirt, sand or snow) that a Tyre suits or doesn't. There are 24, four to a Cup. A Track opens when he beats the Rival on the one before.
_Avoid_: course, level (Level means a kind's difficulty)

**Rival**: one of the computer cars, each with a name, a car and a part of its own. Each Track features one Rival: finishing ahead of it (not coming first) wins its part, the first time, and opens the next Track. A Rival who beats him gets a little slower on that Track each time until he wins.
_Avoid_: enemy, opponent, bot

**Cup**: six groups of four Tracks, one per surface or place: Tarmac, Dirt, Sand, Snow, Night City and Volcano. The fourth Track of a Cup is its Cup Final, against the Cup's champion; beating it there wins the Cup's trophy for his shelf.
_Avoid_: league, championship

**Race number**: the number on his car, 0 to 99, picked free on two wheels, tens and ones.

## Hook and Ladder

Hook and Ladder reuses Math Bots', Rover Code's and Sentence Express's terms where they mean the same thing (Mixed Round, Mastery, Stretch, Bolt, Goal, Paint). It has no Tune-Up and no Meetings. These are its own:

**Game Modes (Hook and Ladder)**: the five kinds of question, about what words mean: Two Words, One Word (compound words: tap the picture two words make, tap where a compound splits, pick the second half that makes a real word), Opposites (with pictures, then spoken words, then in a sentence), Sort It (tap all the things in a group, the one that doesn't belong, what a group is called, and a question about one thing in a group), Endings (-s, -ed and -ing on words that don't change their spelling) and Strong Words (words that mean nearly the same, one stronger: which is bigger, which is the loudest, all three in order). Two Words, One Word and Opposites are open from the start; Sort It opens at Opposites Level 2, Endings at Two Words, One Word Level 2, Strong Words at Sort It Level 2.

**Compound**: a word made of two words whose meaning comes from both (fire truck, rainbow, toothbrush). Never one whose halves mislead (butterfly, ladybug). Its **halves** are the two words.
_Avoid_: word sum, joined word

**Silly half**: a short word offered as a wrong second half at Two Words, One Word Level 3 (rain + dog). It never makes a real word with the first half: rain + coat is real, so coat is never offered beside bow.

**Fair opposite**: a word that is also a fair answer for "the opposite of": cool and chilly for hot. Never offered as a wrong choice beside the opposite.

**Group**: what Sort It sorts into: animals, food, clothes, things that go, body parts. Every thing belongs to exactly one.
_Avoid_: category (fine in the research, not on screen)

**Ending**: -s, -ed or -ing, shown as its own tile that joins the word in a lesson.
_Avoid_: suffix, inflection (fine in the research, not on screen)

**Strong Words set**: two or three words that mean nearly the same, weakest first (whisper, talk, shout). The table is short and hand-checked; the parent reads every set. In a lesson the words sit on a **bar** that grows from small to big and each is said in a voice of matching size.

**Station**: Hook and Ladder's Garage: his vehicle in its bay, the wall of **badges** (with the stars he has won on each), the things he has bought for the Station, the big Call button, and the tray of vehicles and parts.
_Avoid_: garage, firehouse (fine to say, but the screen is the Station)

**Vehicle (Hook and Ladder)**: what he takes to the Call, one at a time, each with its own **trick** there. A **Truck** (fifteen fire engines on the Trucks tab, from the Pumper to the Fire Train, plus the Golden Fire Engine prize) parks on the street and sprays with its hose; the Tower Truck sprays from the top of its boom. An **Aircraft** (the Aircraft tab) is either **hovering** (the Fire Drone, Fire Blimp, Jet Pack, Twin Rotor Helicopter, Sky Crane and Fire Helicopter: it hangs in the air beside the scene, sprays from up there, and its rope ladder reaches any floor; the helicopters have a 🪣 **Drop** button) or a **plane** (the Water Bomber, Air Tanker and Super Jet prize: it flies wherever his finger is and drops the water from above, and tapping someone sends them a **parachute**). The Fire Helicopter moved from Trucks to Aircraft; an old save keeps it.
_Avoid_: truck for an aircraft (a Truck is one kind of vehicle)

**Part (Hook and Ladder)**: one Rung on a slot that changes the Call: the **Engine** (a quicker drive, a faster ladder, and its exhaust: puffs, flames, a rainbow), the **Pump** (how high the water reaches), the **Hose** (how wide the spray is), the **Ladder** (how many floors it climbs) and the **Tank** (how long he sprays before a short refill). The **Nozzle**, **Siren**, **Lights**, **Paint** and **Crew Buddy** (a dalmatian first) change how it sprays, sounds and looks.

**Gadget**: a thing he switches on or off (as many as he likes) that goes on whichever vehicle he takes: the Brass Bell, Fire Flag, Spotlight, Smoke Fan, Party Popper, Turbo Ladder, Water Trailer, Rescue Drone (flies down someone the ladder can't reach), Roof Cannon, Hydrant Hookup (the water never runs out), and the Hero Cape prize.
_Avoid_: upgrade, add-on

**Call**: what a finished Round earns (he starts with one): a short drive with a big Siren button, then a scene from the side where he holds a finger to aim the hose and taps people at windows to send the ladder. It can't be failed: fires never spread, there's no clock, and what his parts can't reach yet the **helpers** finish while he's told which part would reach it. It's used, and paid, only when everything is out.
_Avoid_: mission, level, rescue (the Call is the thing; a rescue is the ladder bringing someone down in it)

**Scene (Hook and Ladder)**: one of the thirty-two Calls, getting taller and stranger: the Trash Bin, the Little House, the Barn, the Car, the Forest Edge, the Bakery, the School, the Tree House, the Ship at the Dock, the Tall Building, the Castle, the Airport, the Lighthouse, the Hotel, the Skyscraper, the Rocket Pad; then the Campfire, the Hay Field, the Pizza Shop, the Toy Store, the Library, the Zoo, the Train Station, the Windmill, the Mountain Cabin, the Hospital, the Dinosaur Museum, the Circus, the Stadium, the Big Forest Fire, the Giant Tower, the Volcano Island. A new one is the next Call; after the first time it gives a **badge** and a building in the Town.

**Star Call**: going back to a scene he has answered (by tapping its building in the Town), hotter than last time: each **star** adds two fires (in windows, on roofs, in trees, on the ground), the second star someone more to save, the third star is at night with fires that **flare** (come back once, smaller, after they're put out). Finishing it wins that star (up to three a scene) and pays 2 Bolts more. The same scene and star are always the same fires.
_Avoid_: replay, hard mode

**Mystery Call**: an endless ladder of Calls made up from its **Level** (`call/scenes.ts` mysteryCall): bigger, taller and with more fires the higher it goes, at night every third Level, with flaring fires from Level 4. It opens once he has answered sixteen Calls, and Call goes there when there are no new ones. It pays 4 Bolts, and every fifth Level wins a **prize**: one of ten things that can't be bought (Fireworks Lights, Super Hero, Diamond paint, Golden Nozzle, Hero Horn, Golden Fire Engine, Golden Crown, Hero Hall, Super Jet, Hero Cape).
_Avoid_: random call, endless mode

**Town (Hook and Ladder)**: a lot for every scene, then the Mystery Call's; a scene he has answered stands there with a thank-you sign and its stars, and tapping it makes it his next Call, as a Star Call. Things he buys for the Town stand in its park. It scrolls.

## Dino Dig

Dino Dig reuses Word Wreckers' terms where they mean the same thing (Pattern, Meeting a Pattern, Sound box) and Math Bots', Rover Code's
and Word Wreckers' (Mixed Round, Mastery, Goal). It shares Word Wreckers' word list (`src/dino-dig/words/list.ts`). It has no
Tune-Up. These are its own:

**Gem**: Dino Dig's Bolt, drawn as a cut green gem, earned and spent exactly as Bolts are, because he digs Gems out of the
ground with the bones, and machine parts don't belong at a dig site. A Round pays them, every Dig has two in its dirt, and the shop
spends them. Every game names its currency in `src/currency/kind.ts`.
_Avoid_: Bolt (in Dino Dig it's always Gems); jewel, crystal (the Crystal Geode and Purple Crystal are Fossils, not Gems)

**Game Modes (Dino Dig)**: the five kinds of reading question: Read It (a word is shown, never said; tap its picture), Hear It (hear a
word, tap the written word), Sound Swap ("This says cat. Make it say cot.": tap the sound to change, then the new one), Real or Silly (two
words; tap the real one) and Read and Find (read a short sentence, tap the picture it says). Read It and Hear It are open from the start;
Sound Swap opens at Hear It Level 2, Real or Silly at Read It Level 2, Read and Find once both Read It and Hear It reach Level 2.

**Silly word**: a made-up word a first grader can sound out under a Pattern (vob, shib, blig, fube, treeb, zorp), offered in Real or
Silly. Never a real word, a name, anything rude, or said like a common word spelled another way; never read aloud.
_Avoid_: nonsense word, fake word (to him)

**One sound apart**: two words of the same number of sounds that differ in just one, and say a different sound there (ship / shop /
chip). Level 3 of Read It and Hear It offers them together; a miss on one shows both stacked with the different box lit.

**Heard alike**: a pair the recorded voice says so nearly alike (pen / pin) that Hear It never offers them together.

**Dig**: what a finished Round earns (he starts with one): a patch of ground at a dig site where he rubs the dirt away, taps rocks to crack
them and drags each bone to the crate. A Dig finds one or two bones, two Gems, and on every other Dig a fossil. It's used only when every
bone is in the crate.
_Avoid_: job, trip, launch

**Dig site**: one of six places to dig (Sandy Creek, Fern Gully, Red Rock Canyon, the Badlands, Volcano Ridge, Frozen Cliffs), two
dinosaurs at each, smallest first. Each needs a stronger Tool.

**Tool**: what he digs with: the Brush (free), Trowel, Shovel, Pickaxe, Jackhammer and Digger. A better one clears more dirt a stroke,
cracks rock in fewer taps, and opens the next dig site.

**Gadget (Dino Dig)**: a bought thing that changes a Dig: the Headlamp, Bone Finder (bones show through the dirt), Gem Finder (Gems show through the dirt), Leaf
Blower, Bone Magnet (loose bones fly to the crate), Water Hose, Dynamite, Dig Dog, Rock Drone and Dig Robot. On once bought, unless he
switches it off.

**Bone**: one part of a dinosaur's skeleton (the skull, the ribs, the leg bones, the tail bones, the plates, the frill, the sail...), four to
six a dinosaur, found in a fixed order with the skull last. It snaps onto the skeleton in the Museum.

**Fossil**: one of thirty finds (Ammonite, Dino Tooth, Dino Egg, Amber...) in every other Dig, kept on the Museum's fossil shelf. Never
bought.
_Avoid_: treasure (that's Word Wreckers' and Treasure Fleet's)

**Museum**: the reward's home and the screen he shows off: the hall where his living dinosaurs walk among the things he bought for it,
a plinth for each of the twelve dinosaurs with its skeleton so far, and the fossil shelf.

**Bring it to life**: what a whole skeleton offers: it grows its skin and he plays as it (the Alive run). A dinosaur brought to life
walks in the Museum hall from then on.

**Alive run**: the action reward: he is a living dinosaur, seen from the side, in the Land he picked: hold to walk, tap to jump, 🦖 to
roar, eating snacks, to his nest. Each dinosaur moves its own way (the long neck reaches high leaves, the T. rex smashes rocks, the
Pteranodon flies). It can't be failed: a bump only wobbles him, and anything bumped three times moves out of the way. Free to replay.

**Looks**: a living dinosaur's Color, Markings (stripes, spots... never "pattern", which is a phonics word here), Hat and Roar, bought
once and worn by one dinosaur at a time, each dinosaur its own.
_Avoid_: pattern (for markings)

**Gear**: bought things that change the Alive run for every dinosaur (Super Jump, Turbo Run, Snack Magnet, Glider Cape, Big Stomp...).

**Land**: where the Alive run goes (Fern Forest free; Sunny Beach, Swamp, Desert, Snowy Hills, Volcano, Night Jungle, Candy Land), each
with its own sky, snack and thing in the way.

## Food Truck

Food Truck reuses Math Bots', Rover Code's and Turbo Tally's terms where they mean the same thing (Mixed Round, Mastery, Stretch, Bolt, Tune-Up, Meeting, Goal, Paint, Slot). These are its own:

**Game Modes (Food Truck)**: the six kinds of question, about needs and wants, goods and services, jobs and spending: Need or Want (tap the need; sort four onto the heart shelf and the star shelf; with only enough for one, which to buy first), Goods and Services (tap the good or the service; sort four into the basket and by the helping hands; who gives a service and who makes a good), Jobs (who does a job; which tool a worker uses; in one picture, who made it and who bought it), Add It Up (the order circled on the menu: how much in all), Enough? _Avoid_: producer, consumer (fine in the research, not on screen)

**Dollar**: the money in the questions, whole dollars only, drawn as $1 coins and $5 and $10 bills, never more than $20. Dollars are never Bolts: nothing in the reward is priced in dollars, and customers don't pay him money. Monster Truck Money owns cents and coin names.

The Taco Truck is free; the others are bought with Bolts and he drives one at a time.

**Part (Food Truck)**: what goes on whichever truck he drives, one per **Slot**: the **Grill** (how many orders cook at once), the **Awning** (how many customers can wait), the **Speaker** (its jingle brings customers sooner), the **Lights** (open the night Spots), and the looks: **Paint**, **Sign**, **Mascot** (on the roof) and **Wheels**. **Helpers** (Bubble Machine, Magic Spatula, Turbo Burner, Robot Helper, Conveyor Belt) change how the Service plays and can be switched off again.

**Service**: the reward he plays with his hands: customers walk up with an order drawn as a picture; he drags the base onto a grill spot, the toppings onto the food, holds to cook it and drags it to the customer. About a minute of customers; then everyone still in line is served and it ends with the count of people he fed. A finished Round earns one (he starts with one). It can't be failed: a wrong topping bounces back to its tray, customers never get cross or leave unfed, and if he stops, a finger points at the next step and then it happens by itself.
_Avoid_: shift, level, game

**Order, Ticket**: what a customer wants, shown in a bubble by their head and again on the **ticket** above the grill spot cooking it; a topping already on is greyed.

**Star (Food Truck)**: one for every customer he feeds. Stars open Spots. They are never spent.
_Avoid_: point, score

**Spot**: where the truck parks for a Service, sixteen of them from the Park to the Moon Base, each with its own sky, ground and things in the background, and some with their own visitors. Each opens at a number of stars; the three night Spots (the Night Market, the Starry Camp, the Moon Base) also need Lights.
_Avoid_: level, map, location

**Regular**: one of forty named customers, four for each truck, each with a favourite order from that truck's toppings. A new one comes to every Service while the truck he drives has one he hasn't met; once fed, their sticker goes in the Sticker Book.

**Sticker Book**: the screen he shows off: a sticker for every Regular (❔ and the truck's food for the ones still to meet), a postcard for every Spot, and his row of trucks wearing his parts.
## Deep Light

Deep Light reuses Math Bots', Rover Code's and Hook and Ladder's terms where they mean the same thing (Mixed Round, Mastery, Stretch,
Goal, Paint, Rung). It has no Tune-Up and no Meetings. These are its own:

**Pearl**: Deep Light's Bolt, drawn as a pearl, earned and spent exactly as Bolts are, because a submarine dives for treasure
from the sea floor, not machine parts. Every game names its currency in `src/currency/kind.ts`.
_Avoid_: Bolt (in Deep Light it's always Pearls)

**Game Modes (Deep Light)**: the six kinds of question, about light and sound (NGSS 1-PS4): Light to See (which cave can you see the
crab in; tap everything that makes its own light; why can't you see it), Through or Not (which lets the light through; sort three by
how much light they let through; what will the wall look like), Shake and Sound (which is loud or quiet; tap the one that is shaking;
what happens when the string stops), Shadows (which shadow is this thing's; where will the shadow be; which lamp made it), Mirrors (which
one bounces the light; turn the mirror to light the gem; two mirrors, or which spot) and Signals (how to say hello far away; copy the
lighthouse's flashes; count the flashes and read the key). Light to See, Through or Not and Shake and Sound are open from the start;
Shadows opens at Through or Not Level 2, Mirrors at Light to See Level 2, Signals once both Mirrors and Shake and Sound reach Level 2.

**Makes its own light**: a light source (the sun, a flashlight, a candle, a campfire, a light bulb, an anglerfish's lure). The moon is
not one, and is never offered in "makes its own light" before Level 3, where it has its own lesson.
_Avoid_: glows (a thing lit up glows too), luminous

**Lets all the light through / lets some light through / blocks the light**: the three things a material can do to a beam, said in
plain words. The school words (transparent, translucent, opaque) are met only in a Level 3 lesson. "Lets some light through" is only
ever wax paper, frosted glass or tissue paper (the hand-checked table).
_Avoid_: see-through, clear (for the "some" ones)

**Lab**: the grid a Mirrors question is played on: a lamp, a gem, and mirrors that turn in steps of an eighth of a turn. Every beam on
it is traced by the same ray code (`science/optics.ts`) that decides the answer.

**Rings**: the circles drawn round a sound, big for loud and small for quiet, so a sound question never depends on the iPad's volume.

**Dock**: Deep Light's Garage: his submarine out of the water, the things he has bought for the Dock along the pier, the map of the
ten zones, the big Dive button, and the tray of parts.
_Avoid_: garage, harbour (fine to say, but the screen is the Dock)

**Part (Deep Light)**: one Rung on a slot that changes the Dive: the **Hull** (how deep he can go: each Rung opens deeper zones), the
**Headlights** (how far and how wide his light reaches), the **Sonar** (how far its ping reaches and how often it goes), the **Motor**
(how fast), the **Claw** (how near treasure must be to grab it) and the **Camera Flash** (lights the whole sea for a moment and
photographs everything near). **Paint**, **Glow Stripes**, **Light Colors**, **Horns**, the **Buddy** in the window and **Bubble
Trails** change how he looks and sounds; **Dock things** stand on the pier.

**Submarine (Deep Light)**: which one he dives in: the free Explorer Sub or one of eleven he buys (Mini, Duck, Bubble, Torpedo, Turtle,
Manta, Whale, Shark, Angler, Octo, Dragon), each with its own **trick** on a Dive. He **builds** each from a **Nose**, **Tail**,
**Fins** and **Top**, and each keeps its own build and looks; the Parts that open zones are shared by all of them.
_Avoid_: boat, ship, vehicle

**Zone**: one of ten places to dive: the Sunlit Reef, the Kelp Forest, the Twilight Zone, the Shipwreck, the Open Ocean, the Midnight
Zone, the Crystal Cave, the Hot Vents, Under the Ice and the Deep Trench. Each opens when his submarine has the parts it needs (a Hull
Rung to go deeper, and for the Shipwreck, the Crystal Cave and Under the Ice a Claw, Sonar or Headlights Rung; the Kelp Forest and the
Open Ocean are shallow but far, so they need a Motor Rung instead).
_Avoid_: level, world

**Dive**: what a finished Round earns (he starts with one): about a minute in a zone's dark sea, steering with a held finger, seeing only
what his headlights touch and the animals that make their own light. Holding the light on an animal takes its **photo**; passing close
to **treasure**, the Claw picks it up (a Pearl each). Then the submarine rises by itself. It can't be failed: nothing chases him and the
edges of the sea only nudge him back. It's used, and paid, only when it ends.
_Avoid_: trip, mission, level

**Ping**: Sonar's sound going out in a ring; everything it passes, lit or not, shows its **outline** for a moment. Sound finding what
light can't.

**Logbook**: every animal he has photographed, a page for each zone; tapping one says its name and one true fact. About seventy-five real
animals, each fact checked against the page in `sea/animals.ts`.
_Avoid_: collection, album

## Sky Writer

Sky Writer reuses Math Bots', Rover Code's and Dino Dig's terms where they mean the same thing (Mixed Round, Mastery, Bolt, Goal, Gear).
It has no Tune-Up. It takes its short words from Dino Dig's word list (`src/dino-dig/words/list.ts`, read only). These are its own:

**Game Modes (Sky Writer)**: the six kinds of handwriting question: Trace It (trace a letter on the writing lines), Where to Start (tap
where a letter starts, which way it goes first, or which line comes second), Which Way (find the letter the right way round among its
backwards and upside-down turns, or the backwards letter in a word), Tall, Small, Tail (how tall a letter is, and which writing sits on the
line), Write It (hear a letter, write it on empty lines) and Write a Word (write a short word, one box a letter). Trace It and Where to
Start are open from the start; Which Way opens at Trace It Level 2, Tall, Small, Tail at Where to Start Level 2, Write It once both reach
Level 2, Write a Word at Write It Level 2.

**Family**: a group of letters that share a first stroke, taught together and met before any is asked (as Sight Squad's word sets and Dino
Dig's Patterns): the straight-line capitals (L T I H E F), the c family (c a d g o q), tall lines and tails (l t i j k), bumps (r n m h b p),
slants (v w x y z), the rest of the small letters (s e f u), the numbers 0 to 9, and the rest of the capitals.
_Avoid_: group, set (to him)

**Stroke**: one line of a letter from where the pen goes down to where it lifts (or turns back along itself): a letter's strokes come in
order, each with its own start and its own way. Stroke words are the shared phrases said for each ("Start at the top. Pull down. Back up
and around."). A lift is between strokes.

**Lane**: how far from a stroke's path his finger may go and still count. It narrows with Trace It's Level; Write It's is wide and hidden.
Going outside it rubs the stroke out; it is never a miss.

**Rub out**: what happens to a stroke that didn't count (a wobble out of the lane, a tap, a scribble away from the letter): its smoke puffs
away and he tries again. Not a miss. A lift partway keeps what he drew, and he can carry on from there.

**Wrong start, wrong way**: the only two misses in the writing kinds: a real try at the letter from the wrong place on it (the bottom of a
tall line, the cross of t first), or along it backwards (a circle going forward instead of back). Either is taught: the little plane flies
the stroke while its words are said.

**Ball-and-stick**: the print style the letters are drawn in (lines and circles, as Zaner-Bloser teaches, matching Andika's single-storey a
and g). A default for the parent to confirm against his school's style.

**Show**: what a finished Round earns (he starts with one): a flight at one of sixteen places, his plane through ten rings in about 40
seconds, Loop and Roll on buttons, then Free Sky. It's used only when the rings are done.
_Avoid_: launch, trip, job, Dig (those are the other games')

**Free Sky**: the end of a Show: the sky is his, and his plane lays smoke only while his finger is down, so he can draw.

**Postcard**: a Free Sky drawing he keeps; it hangs on the Wall over the place it was drawn. Only he can take one down.

**Patch**: the badge on his Wall for each place he has flown a Show at. Flying a new place gives its patch and opens the next place.

**Airfield**: where Bolts buy planes and parts and his plane is built: twelve planes, Rungs of Engine (speed), Wings (how tight it turns),
Smoke Tank (how long its trail is) and Propeller (how quickly it gets up to speed), Smoke Colors and Shapes, Paint, Pilots, Tricks, Gear
and things for the Wall.
_Avoid_: hangar (that's Time Flies'), shop, garage

**Trick**: a stunt the plane flies by itself from a button: Loop and Roll are free; the third button flies the one he picked (Zigzag,
Heart, Spiral, Big Loop, Star, Figure Eight), drawing its shape in smoke.

**Wall**: the screen he shows people: his postcards, his planes, his patches and the things he bought for it.

## Skyline

Skyline reuses Math Bots', Rover Code's and Turbo Tally's terms where they mean the same thing (Mixed Round, Mastery, Stretch,
Tune-Up, Meeting, Goal, Paint). These are its own:

**Brick**: Skyline's Bolt, drawn as a red clay brick, earned and spent exactly as Bolts are, because a city is built from bricks,
not machine parts. Every game names its currency in `src/currency/kind.ts`. A Brick Tower, the Red Brick House and the Red Brick wall
are things he buys with Bricks, never the currency itself.
_Avoid_: Bolt (in Skyline it's always Bricks)

**Game Modes (Skyline)**: the six kinds of question, about equal groups, rows and the start of multiplying: Equal Groups (buses with the
same number of people on each: are they equal, how many buses, how many on each, how many in all, then "4 groups of 5" with no picture),
Windows (a building's front with windows in rows: how many rows, how many in each row, how many windows, the adding sentence that
matches, and the turn-around), Odd or Even (people pairing up for a ride, numbers to 20, doubles), Count By (house numbers along a street:
hundreds to 1,000, tens and fives past 100, then threes and fours), Share It (boxes shared fairly between trucks, then "how many trucks")
and Times (the × sign, said "rows of", then "times" from Level 2). Equal Groups and Windows are open from the start; Odd or Even and Share
It open at Equal Groups Level 2, Count By at Windows Level 2, Times once both Equal Groups and Windows reach Level 3.

**Row**: a line of things that goes across. A building's floors are its rows; a picture never calls an up-and-down line a row.
_Avoid_: column (to him, until Times; he hears "in each row")

**Turn-around**: the same building tipped on its side: 3 rows of 4 becomes 4 rows of 3, and the windows stay the same. Only the
turn-around questions show the two side by side.

**City**: what Bricks build: his city from above on a fixed grid of streets, Block by Block. People walk, traffic runs, and day turns to
night with the windows lighting up.
_Avoid_: town (that's Hook and Ladder's and Word Wreckers')

**District**: one of six pieces of land, each with its own look and special buildings: Old Town, Downtown, Riverside, the Harbour, the
Hill and the Beach. Jobs open them (4, 12, 25, 45 and 70 Jobs); each one's first Block is his the moment it opens.

**Block**: a piece of land the same size as every other, with a street all round it, cut into Plots. After the Districts the city grows
east for ever, one Block at a time, bought with Bricks (a little dearer each time, then flat at 60).

**Plot**: one place in a Block where one building stands: small, wide or large. Standing a building he owns on a plot is free, and so is
moving or swapping it; nothing on a plot is ever lost.

**Floor**: one storey of a tower, a row of windows. A tower is bought one Floor at a time, up to 30; the tallest tower flies the flag.
Every Floor adds people.

**Job**: the reward he drives: about a minute through his own city from above, holding a finger where he wants to go. A bus route, a taxi
ride, a parcel round, the bin lorry, a street sweep, a tram line, an ice cream run, a helicopter tour, a fire call, an ambulance run. A
finished Round earns one (he starts with one). People or parcels come in equal groups at each stop and the count is said as it goes up.
It can't be failed. A named Job (40 of them, "Old Town Loop") is done once each; after them every Job is made from his own streets and
numbered.
_Avoid_: mission, race, trip, launch (those are the other games')

**Fresh street**: the shop's row of four buildings made from parts (a wall colour, windows, a roof, a sign, a number of floors), the same
four until he buys one, when the next appears.

**Population**: everyone who lives in his city (houses, flats and every Floor), shown with his city's rank: Village, Town, City, Big
City, Huge City, Mega City, then Super City 1, 2, 3 and on without end.

## Jetway

Jetway reuses the other games' terms where they mean the same thing (Mixed Round, Mastery, Stretch, Bolt, Tune-Up, Meeting, Goal, Paint, Slot). Airfield is Sky Writer's word for its shop; in Jetway it is only a passenger rank. These are its own:

**Game Modes (Jetway)**: the five kinds of question, about two-digit adding and taking away with carrying and borrowing, and numbers past 100: Boarding (two groups of passengers board one plane: no carrying, then carrying a ten, then totals past 100), Jumbo (seats in blocks of a hundred, rows of ten and single seats: reading them, which digit is in a place, numbers with a zero, which plane holds more and the < > = signs), Unload (bags come off a plane: no borrowing, borrowing a ten, from a round number and from past 100), Ten Hops (ten or a hundred more and less, then a sum in two hops along the runway number line) and Missing Bags (how many more fill the cart: to the next ten, a later ten, any number). Boarding and Jumbo are open from the start; Unload opens at Boarding Level 2, Ten Hops at Jumbo Level 2, Missing Bags at Unload Level 2.

**Seat blocks**: the picture every lesson uses: a block of a hundred seats, a row of ten, and single seats, on a mat with a column for each place (hundreds, tens, ones). A Boarding lesson snaps ten ones into a new row of ten; an Unload lesson breaks a row of ten into ten ones.
_Avoid_: base-ten blocks, flats, rods, cubes (to him)

**Number wheels**: how he answers a sum: one wheel for each digit, turned up or down, then the check. Three wheels only when the answer can pass 100.

**Airport**: the reward he builds, from above: a plot that starts as a grass strip and a shed, where every piece he buys goes in its own fixed place (runways, taxiways, the terminal in stages, Gates, the tower, hangars and the rest). Pieces change the Flight: a longer runway lets bigger planes fly, the tower opens night Routes, the de-icing pad snowy ones, the seaplane dock island ones.
_Avoid_: base, garage, hangar (for the whole airport)

**Gate**: a stand at the terminal where one plane parks. Six are fixed pieces; after the sixth, "Another Gate" can be bought again and again.

**Fleet**: the planes he owns, from the free Two-Seater to the Supersonic Jet, each with its own name and livery. The one he flies is the one he last tapped (or bought) that his runway can take.
_Avoid_: hangar, garage

**Livery**: how one plane is painted, from parts bought once and used on any plane: a body colour, a stripe, a trim colour and a tail art. The airline's mark goes on every plane.

**Flight**: the reward he flies, about a minute: from the gate along the taxiway (from above, a held finger leads the plane), the take-off with the throttle held (from the side), the cruise picking up stars, and the line of lights down to the far runway. A finished Round earns one (he starts with one). It cannot be failed.
_Avoid_: trip, race, mission

**Route**: one of 24 places he flies to, in order, each with its own little airport and weather. Flying a new one opens the next; one that needs a piece of his airport waits until he builds it, and the next is open meanwhile. His best stars and best landing on each are kept.
_Avoid_: level, track

**Charter (Charter Flight)**: the endless Flights, each made on the spot from its number (the town, the weather, the wind, day or night, the stars and a little airport at the far end), the same every time for that number. They open after three Routes; every fifth brings a Charter prize, never sold.
_Avoid_: random flight, endless mode (to him)

**Passengers, rank**: every Flight adds its passengers to the big board; at each milestone the airport takes a new rank (Airstrip, Airfield, Airport, Big Airport, Hub, Big Hub, Mega Hub, then Super Hub 1, 2, 3 and on without end).

**Watch it run**: his airport working by itself, for show: his own planes landing, taxiing to his gates and leaving again, his trucks about. Nothing is paid or used.

## Carrier Deck

Carrier Deck reuses the other games' terms where they mean the same thing (Mixed Round, Mastery, Stretch, Bolt, Tune-Up, Meeting, Goal, Paint, Slot, Rung). Fleet is Jetway's word for its planes; here it means the ships that sail with his carrier. These are its own:

**Game Modes (Carrier Deck)**: the five kinds of question, about story problems within 100 and bar graphs: Deck Log (a one-step story: the end asked, then the change, then the start), Which Sum (hear a story and tap the number sentence that fits; nothing is worked out), How Many More (two ships or two decks: how many more, how many fewer, then "the big ship has 12 more than the small ship"), Two Steps (a story with two things happening, the first step's answer needed for the second) and Mission Board (a bar graph of four squadrons: read one bar, how many more, how many in all). Deck Log and Which Sum are open from the start; How Many More opens at Deck Log Level 2, Two Steps at Which Sum Level 2, Mission Board at How Many More Level 2.

**Story card**: the story on screen, one sentence at a time as it is said, its numbers big; 🔊 says it all again.

**Number sentence**: a sum written across with a box for the number he would find (`38 + ? = 60`). Which Sum's answers are number sentences; a lesson ends by writing one in the story's order.
_Avoid_: equation (to him)

**Bar picture**: the fuller lesson's picture: a long bar for the whole and two shorter bars for the parts, one of them empty (or, comparing, a long bar and a short one with the extra part marked).
_Avoid_: tape diagram, strip diagram (to him)

**Carrier**: the reward he builds: an aircraft carrier from the side, from above and cut away, that starts as a short hull with a bare deck. He buys it longer a Ship Section at a time and fits it out (the island, catapults, landing wires, lifts, the hangar deck and its bays, deck lights, the angled deck and the rest). Parts change the Mission.
_Avoid_: warship, battleship

**Air Wing**: the aircraft he owns, from the free Trainer Jet to the Jump Jet, each with its paint, tail art and number. The hangar's size sets how many he can own; a longer deck takes bigger ones.
_Avoid_: squadron (a Mission Board word), fleet (for aircraft)

**Mission**: the reward he flies, about a minute: the catapult (hold, then let go), a job flown with one finger (rings, following a whale or a ferry, finding lost boats, storm clouds, crate drops, a winch rescue), then the landing lights down to a wire. A finished Round earns one (he starts with one). It cannot be failed: missing the wires sends him round again, and the third pass lands itself. Peaceful: rescues, supplies, storm watches, air shows.
_Avoid_: sortie, attack, strike

**Wire**: one of the landing wires across the stern of the deck; the hook catches one. He aims for the first (the second of four); which one he caught is his landing.

**Patch**: the badge each hand-made Mission puts on his wall, and the prize every fifth generated one may bring.

**Voyage, chart, port**: the sea chart of his voyage: six hand-made places (Home Port, the Islands, the Ice, the Reef, the Storm Sea, the Far Harbor) of six Missions each, then new waters and ports generated ahead of him without end.
_Avoid_: level, map (Treasure Fleet's)

**Radio call**: a generated Mission, made on the spot from its number (the job, the wind, the sea, day or night, which aircraft suits it), the same every time for that number.

**Fleet (Carrier Deck)**: the ships that sail with his carrier: eight to buy (the Tug, the Rescue Cutter, the Supply Ship and the rest), then the Yard's: always a new ship in the Yard, made from parts (hull, deckhouse, mast, funnel, colours), without end.

**Logbook, rank**: every Mission adds its minutes to his logbook; at each milestone he takes a new rank and a new stripe on his jersey (Cadet, Deckhand, Flyer, Pilot, Ace, Flight Leader, Wing Leader, Captain, Commodore, Admiral, then Fleet Admiral 1, 2, 3 and on without end).

**Watch the deck**: his carrier working by itself, for show: his aircraft up the lift, off the catapult, back onto a wire and down again, his Fleet sailing alongside. Nothing is paid or used.

## Cargo Words

Cargo Words reuses the other games' terms where they mean the same thing (Mixed Round, Mastery, Stretch, Bolt, Tune-Up, Meeting, Goal, Slot, Rung, Mod, Paint). Bossy R is Dino Dig's sixth Pattern (ar, or, er, ir, ur); here it is a kind about bossy r inside long words. These are its own:

**Game Modes (Cargo Words)**: the six kinds of question, about reading long words a syllable at a time: How Many Containers (how many beats a word has: heard with its picture, heard, then written and not said), Load the Ship (hear a word, load its syllable containers in order: two exactly, three and a spare, then a spare that looks or sounds close), Split It (tap where a written two-beat word breaks: two small words or twin letters, two different letters, then one letter in the middle, said slowly), Read It (read a long word that is never said, tap its picture: two pictures, three with vowel teams and bossy r, three beats), Bossy R (fill a long word's r container, read a long bossy-r word, load a three-beat one with an r spare) and Add-Ons (un and re at the front, ful, less, er and est at the back: tap the picture, which word means it, which word is hiding inside it, and -er and -est with pictures). How Many Containers and Load the Ship are open from the start; Split It opens at How Many Containers Level 2, Read It and Bossy R at Load the Ship Level 2, Add-Ons at Split It Level 2.

**Container (in the questions)**: a syllable: a coloured shipping container with one syllable written on its side in Andika. A long word is a row of containers. The same container is lifted by the Crane.
_Avoid_: block, tile, chunk (to him)

**Beat**: a syllable, as it is said to him ("How many beats?").
_Avoid_: syllable (to him; the spec and code say syllable)

**Syllable types**: closed (rab), magic e (cake), vowel team (rain), bossy r (gar) and the three that are new here, each met in a Meeting first: open (ti, the vowel says its name), consonant-le (tle) and the final y that says ee (py).

**Port**: the reward he builds, a harbour seen from the front: the land with its buildings, the yard, the rail and the gate, and the quay with his cranes and his ships at their berths. Every piece goes in its own fixed place. Pieces change what he can do: berths take ships, the long and deep-water quays take bigger ships, the crane rails let gantry cranes stand, the lighthouse, fog horn and breakwater open night, foggy and stormy harbours, the dry dock lets him repaint a hull.
_Avoid_: harbour (for the whole port), base, yard (for the whole port)

**Berth**: a place at the quay where one of his ships moors. The wooden pier is the first; Berths 2 to 10 are pieces, then "Another Berth" can be bought again and again. A ship he buys needs a free berth.

**Crane**: the action reward, about a minute, from the side: he drags the crane's trolley along its boom and taps to lower the grab; it locks on by itself, lifts, and he carries each container over the ship and sets it down. A Round earns one go ("a Ship to load"). It cannot be failed and has no clock. Also the five cranes he can own (Dock Crane, Tall Crane, Gantry Crane, Floating Crane, Giant Gantry), each reaching a bigger ship, with parts for its Slots: the grab, the cable, the trolley, lights and a horn.
_Avoid_: claw, game (to him)

**Ship (to load)**: what a finished Round earns: one ship at the quay to load with the Crane. It is his own ship when he picks one (or has just bought one), else a visiting ship made on the spot from the go's number.

**Fleet**: the ships he owns, from the free Tug to the Cruise Liner, each with a name he taps through, a hull paint and a funnel mark.

**Harbour**: a place on the Sea Chart his loaded ship sails to. Twenty-four named ones in order, each sending back a crate (a decoration, a container colour, a stripe or a company mark, never sold); one that needs a piece of his port waits. Past them, chart harbours are made on the spot from their number, without end, each with a flag made from parts for his flag wall.
_Avoid_: level, route, island (for all of them)

**Sea Chart**: the board of harbours, his flag wall, his bests (tallest stack, quickest loading for each number of containers), the tonnage and his Trophies.

**Special crate**: what every fifth go brings back: twelve hand-made prizes, then numbered Lucky Containers made from parts. Never sold.

**Tonnage, port rank**: every container loaded adds to the tonnage counter on the harbour office; at each milestone the port takes a new rank (Wooden Pier, Jetty, Little Port, Port, Busy Port, Big Port, Mega Port, then Super Port 1, 2, 3 and on without end).

**Watch it run**: his port working by itself, for show: his ships coming in to their berths, his cranes unloading them, the train and the lorries carrying containers away. Nothing is paid or used.
